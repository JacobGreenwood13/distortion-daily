1. Senate passes the permitting bill 62 to 33; the House decides next

What happened: The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act on Thursday, November 19, by 62 votes to 33. The bill passed as a substitute amendment to H.R. 2347. Fourteen Democrats and one independent joined most Republicans in voting for it; two Republicans, Josh Hawley of Missouri and Mike Rounds of South Dakota, voted against it. The bill shortens deadlines for federal environmental reviews, narrows the window to challenge project approvals in court, limits the circumstances in which a permit can be withdrawn, and requires data centers to pay the transmission costs tied to their power use. It also gives permitted energy and infrastructure projects certainty that their permits will stand, except in extraordinary circumstances, in cases of legal violations, or under a court order. The bill also eases permitting for Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention facilities, according to the Wall Street Journal. The bill now goes to the House, which must pass the same text, and the president must sign it, before it becomes law. Support fell at each step: the Senate voted 73 to 25 on November 9 to take up the vehicle bill, 70 to 27 on November 16 to close debate, 65 to 31 on November 18 on final cloture, and 62 to 33 on final passage. The sponsors, Senators Shelley Moore Capito, Mike Lee, Sheldon Whitehouse, and Martin Heinrich, introduced the package on September 30 and released a 494-page substitute on November 7.

Incentives:

  • The vehicle: the documented mechanism is the substitute, in which the permitting package rides on H.R. 2347, a House-passed bill on the tax treatment of damages for sexual abuse, so the House must approve any version the Senate passes.
  • The count: the documented mechanism is the vote trajectory, in which support fell from 73 to 25 on November 9 to 62 to 33 on final passage as senators read the text.
  • The permit lock: the documented mechanism is the permit-certainty provision, in which permits in effect stand except in extraordinary circumstances, in cases of legal violations, or under a court order.
  • The data centers: the documented mechanism is the transmission rule, in which data centers must pay all of their associated transmission costs.
  • The calendar: the documented mechanism is the lame-duck window, in which the bill must clear the House and reach the president before the new Congress takes office in January.

2. Fed minutes point to a December rate increase; pending home sales hit a record low

What happened: The Federal Reserve released the minutes of its October 27-28 meeting on November 18. The minutes record a 10-2 vote to hold the federal funds rate at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of a quarter-point increase. The minutes state that almost all participants judged further action to raise the rate would likely be appropriate, and that a couple of participants said an increase of more than a quarter point could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavorable. Staff estimated that personal consumption expenditures inflation reached 3.6 percent in September and core PCE inflation reached 3.1 percent, with unemployment at 4.2 percent. The staff also flagged the rapid growth of debt tied to the artificial intelligence buildout, including off-balance-sheet leasing commitments and circular financing arrangements among AI firms, and almost all participants commented on the risk that returns on AI investment could disappoint. Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC on November 18 that the Fed should raise rates by half a point in December if it does not signal faster tightening in 2027. Markets priced a quarter-point December hike at about 95 percent, according to CME FedWatch. The next meeting is December 8-9. In housing, the National Association of Realtors reported on November 18 that its pending home sales index fell 4.7 percent in October to 63.0, the lowest reading since the series began in 2001 and the second straight monthly record low. Contract signings fell 16.7 percent from a year earlier. Existing-home sales fell 5.5 percent in October to a 3.75 million annual rate, the slowest since August 2010, while the median price was $416,700, up 0.4 percent from a year earlier. The 30-year mortgage averaged 7.33 percent in October, up from 6.86 percent in September and 6.25 percent a year earlier. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun said annual existing-home sales could be the lowest since the mid-1990s. The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported consumer prices rose 0.4 percent in October, and employers added 165,000 jobs in October, with unemployment at 4.1 percent.

Incentives:

  • The signal: the documented mechanism is the minutes, in which the published record of almost all participants expecting further hikes moved market pricing to about 95 percent for a quarter-point December increase.
  • The dissent: the documented mechanism is the vote, in which two officials voted for an immediate increase and a couple more raised a larger December move, showing the committee's center had shifted toward tightening.
  • The AI channel: the documented mechanism is the buildout, in which staff tied part of the inflation pressure to AI data center spending and flagged opaque financing structures that could amplify losses if returns disappoint.
  • The housing channel: the documented mechanism is the mortgage rate, in which a 7.33 percent average 30-year rate pushed contract signings to the lowest level since the series began in 2001.
  • Structural note: the minutes do not name which officials took which positions in the discussion.

  • Associated Press, November 18 — nearly all officials expect a quarter-point December hike, Waller told CNBC the Fed should raise rates by half a point in December, CME FedWatch near 95 percent

3. Senate rejects the Iran war powers resolution, 48 to 51

What happened: The Senate voted 48 to 51 on Thursday, November 19, against adopting H.Con.Res. 93, a House-passed concurrent resolution that would have directed the president to remove United States armed forces from hostilities against Iran unless Congress declared war or passed a specific authorization for military force. The resolution invoked section 5(c) of the War Powers Resolution. The House passed it on September 15 by 220 votes to 204. Three Republicans voted for the measure: Susan Collins of Maine, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska, and Rand Paul of Kentucky. One Democrat, John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, voted against it. Senator Chris Murphy of Connecticut did not vote. The vote was roll call 269 and was the first vote on the war in either chamber since the November 3 midterm elections. The war with Iran began on February 28. When the new Congress takes office in January, Democrats will hold 56 Senate seats and an 83-seat House majority, margins that would be enough to approve another war-ending resolution.

Incentives:

  • The rule: the documented mechanism is section 5(c), in which a concurrent resolution can direct the removal of forces, but it requires both chambers, so the Senate vote ended this resolution.
  • The crossovers: the documented mechanism is the vote count, in which three Republicans voted yes and one Democrat voted no, leaving the measure three votes short of a majority.
  • The calendar: the documented mechanism is the new Congress, in which a 56-seat Democratic Senate majority and an 83-seat House majority take office in January with the numbers to approve another resolution.
  • Structural note: the vote rejected a particular withdrawal resolution; it adopted no authorization for military force.

Sources:

  • Reuters, November 19 — Senate voted 51 to 48 to defeat the war powers resolution, largely along party lines, weeks after midterm defeats tied to the conflict
  • Senate Democratic Caucus, Wrap Up for Thursday, November 19, 2026 — H.Con.Res.93, Iran War Powers, not agreed to, 48-51
  • USA Today, November 19 — Collins, Murkowski, and Paul voted for the measure, Fetterman was the only Democrat to oppose it, first vote on the war since the midterms
  • The Financial, November 19 — H.Con.Res.93, House passed September 15 by 220-204, roll call 269, Murphy did not vote, the vote supplied no new statutory authorization for military action

4. Serbia elects Cupic speaker, 140 to 97; he becomes acting president

What happened: Serbia's National Assembly elected Aleksandar Cupic, a university lecturer from the Students' List, as its speaker on Thursday, November 19, by 140 votes to 97. His opponent was Stojan Radenovic, nominated by the parliamentary group led by former President Aleksandar Vucic. Two deputies abstained, and 239 deputies took part in the vote. The vote followed three days of debate at the constitutive session of the 15th parliament, which was constituted on November 17 after the mandates of all 250 members were confirmed. It was the first contested speaker election since 2012. As speaker, Cupic also becomes acting president of the republic and must call a presidential election by November 27; the election must be held by December 27. The assembly also elected six deputy speakers, all from the Students' List, and appointed Mirjana Radakovic as secretary general. The Students' List won 140 of the 250 seats in the October 25 parliamentary election, ending 14 years of rule by the Serbian Progressive Party. The assembly has 90 days, until February 15, 2027, to elect a government.

Incentives:

  • The seat count: the documented mechanism is the majority, in which the Students' List holds 140 of 250 seats, enough to elect the speaker without support from other groups.
  • The office: the documented mechanism is the speakership, in which the speaker also serves as acting president and controls the call for the presidential election.
  • The deadline: the documented mechanism is the 90-day clock, in which the assembly must elect a government by February 15, 2027.
  • Structural note: electing a speaker is a procedural step; it does not itself form a government.

5. Anthropic raises $81.3 billion in the largest IPO on record; stocks fall 2 percent

What happened: Anthropic, the maker of the Claude artificial intelligence models, began trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday, November 19, under the ticker ANT, after pricing its initial public offering at $83 a share. The company sold 980 million shares and raised about $81.3 billion, the largest initial public offering on record, passing SpaceX's June listing. Shares opened at $111.50, about 34 percent above the offer price, and closed at $97.17, up 17 percent from the offer price. At one point during the day the company's value passed $2 trillion. About 15 percent of the shares in the offering went to retail investors, according to the Wall Street Journal. The Securities and Exchange Commission declared the registration statement effective on November 18. On the same day, the broader market fell: the Nasdaq Composite dropped about 2 percent, the S&P 500 fell 2 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 2 percent to 49,097. Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices, and Alphabet all fell.

Incentives:

  • The gap: the documented mechanism is the allocation, in which institutions and large investors bought at the $83 offer price while the stock opened to the public at $111.50, a 34 percent first-trade premium.
  • The record: the documented mechanism is the size, in which 980 million shares at $83 raised about $81.3 billion, passing SpaceX's June listing as the largest offering on record.
  • The split: the documented mechanism is the market move, in which Anthropic closed up 17 percent from its offer price on the same day the Nasdaq fell about 2 percent and the S&P 500 fell 2 percent.
  • Structural note: the registration statement was declared effective on November 18, one day before trading began.

6. First US-China-Russia trilateral ends without a Ukraine deal; Russia strikes Kyiv overnight

What happened: President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin held the first trilateral meeting of the leaders of the United States, China, and Russia on Thursday, November 19, in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. Trump also met Putin separately for about 40 minutes and discussed the war in Ukraine. The three leaders issued a joint declaration pledging dialogue on reducing nuclear risks, maintaining stable energy supplies, and reforming the United Nations. The declaration made no mention of Ukraine and announced no peace initiative. Putin rejected ceasefire discussions and warned of a harsher Russian response after Ukrainian strikes on the Kerch Strait Bridge. Ukrainian forces struck the bridge a third time in four days overnight on November 19, targeting its rail section with Neptun missiles; Russian authorities suspended train traffic and reported no casualties. Hours after the Shenzhen meeting, Russia launched a mass overnight missile and drone attack on Ukraine. Ukrainian officials said Russia fired more than 110 missiles, including 27 ballistic missiles, and more than 400 drones, including 120 jet-powered drones. At least eight people were killed and 28 injured across Ukraine, including six killed in Kyiv. Three bridges across the Dnipro River in Kyiv were damaged, and power outages spread across the capital and western regions; the grid operator Ukrenergo called the situation in the power grid extremely critical. Ukraine's air force said it downed 380 drones, 79 cruise missiles, and three ballistic missiles. The trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi between Ukraine, Russia, and the United States concluded this week with a prisoner-exchange agreement and no agreement on territory; Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said Russia would control all territory it claims.

Incentives:

  • The table: the documented mechanism is the guest list, in which the summit was proposed as a way to discuss the war in Ukraine without Ukrainian representatives at the table.
  • The bridge: the documented mechanism is the supply line, in which the Kerch Strait Bridge carries Russian logistics between Russia and occupied Crimea; its road span is down and rail traffic was suspended again after the third strike.
  • The timing: the documented mechanism is the sequence, in which the mass attack on Kyiv followed the Shenzhen meeting by hours and followed Putin's warning of a harsher response.
  • The energy target: the documented mechanism is the grid, in which strikes on power plants and transmission substations forced emergency outages in Kyiv and western regions as temperatures fell below freezing.
  • Structural note: the joint declaration listed immunity for state officials as a matter of fundamental importance; the International Criminal Court has issued an arrest warrant for Putin.
