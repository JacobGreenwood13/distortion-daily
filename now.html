1. Peltola 157 votes short of a majority in Alaska; ranked-choice count set for November 18

What happened: The Alaska Division of Elections' November 14 count showed Democrat Mary Peltola with 140,637 first-choice votes (49.94 percent) and Republican Senator Dan Sullivan with 129,424 (45.96 percent), a lead of 11,213 votes out of 281,587 cast in the race. Peltola sits 157 votes short of the 140,794 needed for a majority of the votes cast. Republican Gerald Heikes holds 6,002 votes (2.13 percent) and Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired teacher from Petersburg, holds 4,652 (1.65 percent), with 872 write-in votes. All 401 precincts have reported, and 283,618 of 605,433 registered voters cast ballots. The Associated Press has not called the race. Peltola's campaign declared victory on November 12; Sullivan has not conceded. FairVote's preliminary ranked-choice simulation, run on the cast-vote record updated November 13, gives Peltola the final round 51.5 percent to 48.5 percent, a margin of 8,034 votes. The Juneau Independent reported on November 14 that fewer than 12,000 ballots remained to be counted. The Division of Elections runs the ranked-choice tabulation on Wednesday, November 18, and aims to certify the result on November 25. Alaska voters also rejected Ballot Measure 2, which would have repealed the top-four open primary and ranked-choice voting: No led with 144,098 votes (53.62 percent) to 124,631 Yes (46.38 percent), and the Associated Press called the measure as failed on Friday evening. A repeal attempt failed by fewer than 800 votes in 2024. In the governor's race, Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins won 141,623 votes (50.90 percent), a majority of first choices. In the House race, Republican Representative Nick Begich leads with 133,707 votes (47.71 percent) to 116,932 (41.72 percent) for Bill Hill.

Incentives:

  • The majority gap: the documented mechanism is the 50-percent threshold, in which a candidate with no majority of first choices goes to ranked-choice tabulation, scheduled for November 18.
  • The simulation: the documented mechanism is the cast-vote record, in which FairVote ran the ranked-choice rounds on about 274,000 ballots and found Peltola ahead 51.5 percent to 48.5 percent; the simulation is preliminary, not an official result.
  • The measure: the documented mechanism is Ballot Measure 2, in which voters rejected repeal of the same ranked-choice system that will decide the Senate race, by 53.62 percent to 46.38 percent.
  • Structural note: race calls are projections based on counted votes and remaining ballots, not certifications; the Associated Press has called the measure and not the Senate race.

2. Zelenskyy says Washington offered NATO membership for a full Donbas withdrawal; Kostyantynivka ceasefire disputed

What happened: Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Saturday, November 14, that the United States delegation at the November 10 and 11 talks in Abu Dhabi proposed Ukraine gain NATO membership in exchange for a Ukrainian withdrawal from the entirety of the Donbas region, according to the Institute for the Study of War. Zelenskyy also responded on November 14 to the Kremlin's proposal for a humanitarian ceasefire in Kostyantynivka, saying Moscow would use a ceasefire to force Ukrainian forces to leave the town, seize it, and advance toward Kramatorsk and Slovyansk. Zelenskyy cited satellite imagery and said Russian forces used the ceasefire in and near occupied Oleshky, Kherson Oblast, in late October to bring up weapons and strengthen their positions. Ukraine gave the United States a list of seven or eight occupied settlements that would benefit from a humanitarian ceasefire; the Kremlin rejected those alternatives. The Institute for the Study of War assessed that Russian forces do not control Kostyantynivka, citing evidence of at least 41 Ukrainian positions in and around the town since September 1, against Russian claims that its forces have controlled the city since July. The Ukrainian General Staff reported that Russian forces conducted 47 attacks in the Dobropillya direction on November 13 and 14, the largest number in that direction since November 1. Ukrainian officials reported disruptions to SpaceX's Starshield satellite communications services since November 10, with all Ukrainian missions starting to fail on November 13; SpaceX denies the disruptions. Zelenskyy also said Europe must decide by the end of November who will represent it in the talks, and he hopes for a decision at a Coalition of the Willing meeting in Germany on November 19. European diplomats attended the Abu Dhabi talks but sat outside the three-way sessions; they included Jonathan Powell of Britain, Günter Sautter of Germany, Emmanuel Bonne of France, and Fabrizio Saggio of Italy. European Union foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said the treaties give the seat to European Council President Antonio Costa and herself; German Chancellor Friedrich Merz named the E3 and a group of five; French President Emmanuel Macron said the format "will be determined" later.

Incentives:

  • The offer: the documented mechanism is the exchange as Zelenskyy described it, in which NATO membership is set against withdrawal from the whole Donbas; Zelenskyy has said Ukraine will not surrender occupied territory.
  • The ceasefire: the documented mechanism is the Oleshky precedent, in which a late-October ceasefire covered the wider Kherson direction to evacuate civilians, and Zelenskyy says satellite imagery shows Russia used it to move weapons and strengthen positions.
  • The Europe seat: the documented mechanism is the representation question, in which Zelenskyy set an end-of-November deadline and a November 19 meeting in Germany as the target for a decision.
  • Structural note: the Institute for the Study of War assesses Russian forces do not control Kostyantynivka; Russian officials claim control since July.

3. Huang weighs a return to Nvidia as Bessent demands an outside CEO and answers in 10 days

What happened: Jensen Huang is considering a return as Nvidia's chief executive and has discussed the possibility with confidants, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, November 15, citing people familiar with the matter. One ally told the newspaper that supporters were assembling a group of Silicon Valley executives, described as a "war room," to lobby Nvidia's board for Huang's reinstatement and to seek backing from the White House. The same day, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Fox News's "Sunday Morning Futures" that Nvidia needs "a dramatic shake-up" and that its board should look outside the company for its next leader. Bessent said he wants answers within 10 days from the board and its outside counsel on the company's compliance with United States export restrictions, including whether it pushed "seemingly illicit sales" of artificial intelligence chips to China and questions about its Singapore sales disclosures, according to Reuters and Al Jazeera. Huang resigned as president, chief executive officer, and board member on November 12, effective immediately; the board had accepted the resignation on November 11. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress serves as interim chief executive and keeps the finance job. Shares closed on Friday, November 13, at $162.85, down 2.0 percent, after falling about 12.5 percent over the week. Nvidia reports third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17, with Kress leading the earnings call; the company's outlook calls for revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2 percent, and analysts expect adjusted earnings per share of $2.49, according to Barchart. A "Stand with Jensen" petition has drawn more than 160,000 signatures, according to CNBC, and Altimeter Capital chief executive Brad Gerstner posted a letter on Friday urging the board to run a slow, deliberate process including "the potential return of Jensen Huang." The board's independent members have hired the law firm Wachtell Lipton and former federal prosecutor Joon Kim to investigate Huang's conduct. Three House committee chairs required closed-door transcribed interviews — Huang on December 1 and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter on December 2 — and Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott asked Huang to testify on December 3 and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on December 10. Nvidia has issued no statement since the November 12 resignation announcement and did not respond to the Financial Times report. Headhunters have begun scouting external candidates, naming Arm chief executive Rene Haas and Advanced Micro Devices chief executive Lisa Su, according to The Standard.

Incentives:

  • The board: the documented mechanism is the bylaws, in which only the board can appoint a chief executive; it has issued no statement since November 12 and has not said whether it would consider Huang again.
  • The Treasury pressure: the documented mechanism is the 10-day window, in which Bessent set about November 25 for the board and its outside counsel to answer questions on export-control compliance.
  • The earnings: the documented mechanism is the November 17 report, in which the first results under Kress test the business against a $108 billion revenue outlook.
  • The petition: the documented mechanism is the signature count, in which more than 160,000 people signed a petition for Huang's reinstatement, with Tesla chief executive Elon Musk, Epic Games chief executive Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator chief executive Garry Tan listed among supporters, affiliations self-reported and not verified.
  • Structural note: the comeback account comes from the Financial Times, as reported by Reuters; Nvidia has not confirmed it.

4. Six more tankers claimed hit in the Strait of Hormuz; oil slips as the IEA calls a fourth-quarter surplus

What happened: Iran's navy and the Revolutionary Guards said three tankers caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, November 14, and Iran's navy said on Sunday night that an oil tanker caught fire after striking a naval mine east of the strait. On Monday, November 16, the Revolutionary Guards said two more oil tankers struck mines in the strait and exploded, and Reuters reported six tankers caught fire in similar incidents on Sunday and Monday. Iran named none of the vessels, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the reports. The United States military says it has already cleared the main shipping routes of mines. Brent crude settled at $95.84 a barrel on Friday, November 13, and fell 47 cents to $95.37 on Monday; United States West Texas Intermediate fell 23 cents to $88.33. The International Energy Agency said on Friday that the world oil market will return to a surplus in the fourth quarter of 2026, with supply averaging 105 million barrels per day, 1.7 million barrels per day higher than forecast demand, as Middle East exports recover faster than global oil demand despite continued attacks on shipping. The agency said 71 million barrels from a record release of its members' emergency reserves would be made available by the end of February, including 15 million barrels of diesel. President Donald Trump extended for 90 days a waiver of the Jones Act, which requires United States-flagged ships to carry cargo between United States ports, a White House official said on Friday. About 15 million barrels per day of crude oil moved through the Strait of Hormuz in 2025, according to International Energy Agency data.

Incentives:

  • The claims: the documented mechanism is the unnamed vessels, in which Iran disclosed no ship names for the tankers it says were hit, and no independently confirmed strike has been reported since the Dominica Prosperity on November 11.
  • The surplus: the documented mechanism is the fourth-quarter balance, in which the International Energy Agency forecasts supply 1.7 million barrels per day above demand.
  • The waiver: the documented mechanism is the Jones Act waiver, in which the 90-day extension lets foreign-flagged ships move oil and fuels between United States ports.
  • The floor: the documented mechanism is the shipping rate, in which rates remain high because of the risks tankers face in the strait and the number of ships tied up shuttling oil through the waterway, putting a floor under prices.
  • Structural note: Iran's claims have not been independently confirmed; the United States military says the main routes are clear of mines.

5. Defund-the-war amendment filed as Democrats split on using the December 11 deadline

What happened: Senators Tammy Duckworth of Illinois, Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, and Kirsten Gillibrand of New York announced on Friday, November 13, that they will offer an amendment to any government funding deal that would defund President Donald Trump's war in Iran until Congress authorizes the conflict. The amendment blocks new dollars for offensive operations in Iran while allowing troops to defend themselves, and it withholds travel funding for the Secretary of Defense until the Department of Defense produces a report on the war's impact on military readiness and servicemembers. Duckworth said she will vote against the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2027 unless her amendment to end the war is included. The amendment follows the November 12 joint statement by Senators Michael Bennet of Colorado, Duckworth, Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, and Bernie Sanders of Vermont, an independent who caucuses with Democrats, that they will not accept a short-term spending patch without language restricting the administration from using appropriated funds to continue the war. Senator Tim Kaine of Virginia is drafting cutoff language for the next continuing resolution. Senator Patty Murray of Washington, the top Democrat on the Senate Appropriations Committee, said Democrats are "still going to have language stopping this war," according to Roll Call, while also saying leaders were debating how long the stopgap should last and whether to add restrictions, and that "we're exploring all options at this point." Murray has endorsed a short-term continuing resolution that would fund the government into the new year, and was noncommittal on whether it would include war limits. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said he did not want to get ahead of bipartisan negotiations and deferred to Representative Rosa DeLauro of Connecticut, the top Democrat on House Appropriations. President Donald Trump said on Thursday that he would not seek a deal to formally end the conflict: "We don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed." The Office of Management and Budget has not sent Congress its requested changes for a stopgap, and the administration is considering a bill running through March, according to Roll Call. Federal agencies are funded through December 11 under Public Law 119-103, and none of the 12 annual appropriations bills for fiscal 2027 has been enacted. Any short-term bill needs 60 Senate votes; Republicans hold 53 seats, so at least seven Democratic votes are required. Without a new law, a funding lapse begins on December 12. The war has cost the United States military $47.1 billion as of October 16, according to figures United States Central Command provided to Congress.

Incentives:

  • The amendment: the documented mechanism is the funding condition, in which the amendment attaches war-funding limits to the bill that keeps the government open past December 11.
  • The votes: the documented mechanism is the 60-vote threshold, in which Republicans hold 53 seats and need at least seven Democratic votes to advance a funding bill.
  • The split: the documented mechanism is the timing question, in which Murray has endorsed a stopgap running into the new year while remaining noncommittal on whether it carries war limits, and Kaine is drafting cutoff language for the December bill.
  • The defense bill: the documented mechanism is the authorization, in which Duckworth conditions her vote for the fiscal 2027 defense bill on the inclusion of her amendment.
  • Structural note: the amendment is a proposal by three senators, not a chamber vote; no stopgap text has been introduced.

6. Knesset speaker vote set for November 17 as Eisenkot says Netanyahu "cannot remain" prime minister

What happened: The 26th Knesset was sworn in on Tuesday, November 10, after the October 27 general election left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud alliance with 53 of 120 seats and Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party and its partners with 48. President Isaac Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government after Eisenkot secured the backing of 67 members of the Knesset, compared with 46 for Netanyahu. Eisenkot has until December 6 to assemble a coalition and can seek a two-week extension to December 20; if both Eisenkot and Netanyahu fail, another election could follow. Eisenkot's bloc chose former minister Matan Kahana of Yashar as its candidate for Knesset speaker to replace Amir Ohana of Likud, and 67 lawmakers signed a request to remove Ohana, who scheduled the vote for Tuesday, November 17. Negotiators from Yashar, Likud, and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad party met for an hour at the Dan Accadia Hotel in Herzliya on Sunday, November 15, to discuss a unity government; the meeting produced no progress. Eisenkot said Netanyahu cannot remain prime minister amid allegations of vote meddling, Al Jazeera and France 24 reported. The Times of Israel reported on Sunday that police are said to be examining whether Netanyahu's office faked an Arab vote-buying scandal in order to undermine the election; the reported inquiry has not been independently confirmed. Negotiations between Eisenkot's bloc and United Torah Judaism were reported to be advancing significantly, and Netanyahu is pressing the party to oppose Kahana's appointment. B'Yachad lawmaker Liran Avisar Ben Horin and Yisrael Beytenu leader Avigdor Liberman take up their posts as chairs of the Finance Committee and the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee on Monday, November 16. On Wednesday, the bloc plans to advance bills repealing legislation affecting the attorney general's authority and the composition of the Judicial Selection Committee. The leader of the Yesh Atid party dismissed Netanyahu's public call for unity negotiations as a political "bluff" on Saturday evening, saying talks between the negotiating teams had already been scheduled for the following day.

Incentives:

  • The speaker: the documented mechanism is the chair, in which controlling the speakership lets Eisenkot's bloc advance legislation while coalition talks continue.
  • The deadline: the documented mechanism is the mandate clock, in which Eisenkot has until December 6, with a possible extension to December 20, before another election becomes possible.
  • The ultra-Orthodox parties: the documented mechanism is the coalition arithmetic, in which United Torah Judaism's support would lift a prospective cabinet to 55 seats, six short of a majority.
  • Structural note: the police examination of the prime minister's office is a reported development, not an established finding; the vote-meddling allegation has not been independently confirmed.
