1. Democrats win at least 258 House seats, the largest majority since 1992

What happened: The Associated Press called the race in North Carolina's 5th Congressional District on Sunday, November 22, for Democrat Chuck Hubbard over Republican Representative Virginia Foxx. A recount confirmed Hubbard's lead of 330 votes out of about 287,000 ballots cast, and Foxx conceded on Sunday. Hubbard's win gave Democrats at least 258 seats in the 435-member House, with four races still uncalled. Democrats gained 44 House seats in the November 3 midterm elections, four more than the 40 they picked up in 2018. The 258-seat total matches the number Democrats won in 1992 and exceeds the 257 they won in 2008. Foxx was first elected in 2004 and chaired the House Rules Committee. Democrats will hold both chambers when the new Congress convenes in January, with a Republican president in the White House.

Incentives:

  • The margin: the documented mechanism is the seat count, in which 258 seats gives Democrats a majority of more than 40 seats, enough to pass legislation without Republican votes.
  • The recount: the documented mechanism is North Carolina law, in which a margin under 1 percent allows the trailing candidate to request a recount.
  • The committee: the documented mechanism is the Rules Committee, in which the chair controls which bills reach the House floor and under what terms; Foxx's loss removes a Republican from that post.
  • The calendar: the documented mechanism is the January convening, in which the new Congress takes office on January 3, 2027, and Democrats take control of both chambers.
  • Structural note: four races remained uncalled, so the final total could rise above 258.

2. Iran attacks Bahrain; Trump signals strikes could resume after the New Year

What happened: Bahrain said on Saturday, November 21, that an Iranian attack hit a civilian industrial facility, starting a fire. Bahrain's Interior Ministry said two people were injured, a Pakistani resident in critical condition and a Bahraini citizen in stable condition. Bahrain's Defence Force said its air defences intercepted a number of Iranian aerial attacks and that falling shrapnel caused material damage. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they launched a missile-and-drone assault on US military positions at the Al-Riffa and Awali bases in Bahrain, home to the headquarters of the US Navy's Fifth Fleet. The Guards claimed they struck two US Patriot radar systems; US Central Command had denied similar claims earlier in the week. It was the fourth Iranian attack on Bahrain in nine days. On Friday, November 20, President Donald Trump said Iran wants a deal to end hostilities but not one that is acceptable to him. On Sunday, November 22, Trump told Fox News he was inclined to order renewed military strikes after the new year if negotiations fail, and warned Iranian vessels testing the US naval blockade would end up at the bottom of the sea. The Financial Times reported on November 20 that US and Israeli officials are pressing Trump to resume strikes before the new year, while military planners warned the build-up could become hard to sustain. Federal funding runs out on December 11 under P.L. 119-103, and the new Congress takes office on January 3, 2027.

Incentives:

  • The blockade: the documented mechanism is the naval blockade, in which US forces stop Iranian crude exports, cutting Tehran's oil revenue.
  • The bases: the documented mechanism is the target list, in which the Guards named US military positions in Bahrain, putting the headquarters of the Fifth Fleet inside the exchange.
  • The deadline: the documented mechanism is the funding law, in which federal funding expires on December 11, giving the incoming Congress a vote on war funding before any decision on new strikes.
  • The Senate vote: the documented mechanism is the war powers vote, in which the Senate rejected the withdrawal resolution 48 to 51 on November 19, leaving the president's authority unchanged.
  • Structural note: the Guards' claims of hits on US equipment were not independently verified; Bahrain confirmed only the industrial facility fire and the two injuries.

3. US pushes Ukraine deal by early January as Russia strikes the Kyiv region and missiles land in Poland

What happened: The Financial Times reported on November 20 that the Trump administration told European and Ukrainian officials it wants a peace agreement by early January, before the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Russian, Ukrainian, and European officials in Moscow, Dublin, and Berlin during the week. Kushner told European leaders in Berlin on November 19 that Moscow was comfortable with an Article 5-style security guarantee for Ukraine, a guarantee modelled on NATO's clause that an attack on one member is treated as an attack on all. Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov said on November 20 that Moscow rejects any form of Ukraine's quasi-membership in NATO. President Volodymyr Zelensky said on November 21 that the United States proposed holding trilateral talks before Christmas, possibly on the sidelines of the G20 summit in Miami on December 14-15; Trump has invited Russian President Vladimir Putin to the summit but has not yet invited Ukraine. On the ground, Russia struck the town of Dymer in the Kyiv region on November 22; the death toll reached 11 on November 23 as rescuers pulled bodies from the rubble of an apartment building. On November 20, during a mass Russian attack on Ukraine involving more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles, two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone landed in southeastern Poland. Polish prosecutors said on November 22 that debris from Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles was recovered at the three crash sites. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said in Warsaw on November 21 that NATO will defend every inch of Polish territory; the North Atlantic Council meets in Brussels on November 23 to discuss the incident.

Incentives:

  • The clock: the documented mechanism is the January 3 convening, in which the new Democratic Congress takes office and the administration's window to set terms closes.
  • The guarantee: the documented mechanism is the Article 5-style proposal, in which a collective-defence commitment would bind the United States and its allies to respond to a renewed Russian attack; Moscow has rejected it.
  • The venue: the documented mechanism is the G20 summit, in which a leaders' meeting in Miami on December 14-15 would put the talks on a fixed date before Christmas.
  • The strikes: the documented mechanism is the targeting, in which Russian attacks on the Kyiv region continued through the talks, and Russian weapons crossed into NATO territory during the same barrage.
  • Structural note: no agreement on territory or guarantees has been announced; the Abu Dhabi talks ended with a prisoner-exchange agreement only.

4. COP31 ends without a climate agreement

What happened: The 31st United Nations Climate Change Conference, COP31, ended in Antalya, Turkey, on Sunday, November 22, without agreement on the cover decision, the framework text meant to set the conference's political direction. It was the first COP since 2009 in Copenhagen to end without a final agreement, and the longest COP in history. The talks began on November 9 and ran into record overtime. The draft text contained no mention of fossil fuels and promoted carbon capture technology. The European Union, small island states, and other vulnerable nations said they could not accept a text that failed to mention oil, gas, and coal. Saudi Arabia, China, and Russia supported the text. Around 130 countries signed a letter demanding any deal mention the transition away from fossil fuels. COP31 president Murat Kurum said in the closing plenary that he could see there was no agreement and would not advance a new text. It was the second climate conference since the United States withdrew from the UN climate process, and the first since a UN report confirmed the world is set to breach the 1.5°C warming threshold. The European Commission said on November 23 that the EU was disappointed but would keep pushing the multilateral process. Australian Energy Minister Chris Bowen, who served as president of negotiations, faced calls for his resignation at home.

Incentives:

  • The veto: the documented mechanism is consensus, in which agreement among 194 parties is required, so a bloc defending fossil-fuel language can block the text.
  • The absence: the documented mechanism is US withdrawal, in which the absence of active US engagement and close US-China coordination removed a broker that had produced compromises at earlier COPs.
  • The letter: the documented mechanism is the coalition letter, in which about 130 countries demanded fossil-fuel transition language, setting a floor the final text did not meet.
  • The threshold: the documented mechanism is the 1.5°C limit, in which a UN report confirmed the world is set to pass the warming threshold scientists link to intensifying extreme weather.
  • Structural note: the cover decision is a political statement; it does not itself set emissions targets.

Sources:

  • Reuters, November 22 — COP31 set to collapse without a political agreement, Kurum said there would be no new draft, multiple blocs rejected the text
  • Reuters, November 23 — Saudi Arabia resisted fossil-fuel language, first failure to reach a political agreement since 2009, absence of US engagement strengthened Riyadh's hand
  • Reuters, November 23 — EU and others rejected a final deal with no mention of fossil fuels, first meeting since the UN confirmed temperatures set to exceed 1.5°C, Bowen blamed for the failure
  • Le Monde, November 22 — countries rejected the Antalya Pact on November 22, longest COP in history, biggest setback since COP15 in 2009, text supported by Russia, China, and Arab countries
  • Reuters, November 23 — EU disappointed by the collapse, will keep pushing the multilateral process

5. Stocks post their worst week since April 2025; a December rate hike is priced near 98 percent

What happened: US stocks fell sharply on Friday, November 20. The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent to 6,910.39, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3 percent to 22,617.73, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points to 48,147.33. For the week, the S&P 500 lost 8.6 percent, its worst week since April 2025; the Nasdaq lost 10 percent, its worst week since March 2020; and the Dow lost 8 percent. The S&P 500 has now fallen more than 10 percent from its October 6 record, the definition of a correction, and the Nasdaq turned negative for the year. The index fell for seven straight days, the longest losing streak since February 2020. Anthropic shares fell on Friday after rising 17 percent in their Thursday debut, and Nvidia's earnings, reported November 17, failed to steady the market. The sell-off centred on artificial intelligence and chip stocks, as investors questioned whether the returns on AI infrastructure spending justify the borrowing behind it, a concern the Federal Reserve's staff flagged in the October meeting minutes. Markets priced a December rate increase at about 98 percent, according to CME FedWatch. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson said on November 19 that the Fed will need to raise interest rates again because inflation is too high. The Fed's next meeting is December 8-9.

Incentives:

  • The rate path: the documented mechanism is the futures market, in which CME FedWatch pricing near 98 percent reflects traders' expectation of a December increase.
  • The AI channel: the documented mechanism is the buildout, in which AI data-centre spending and opaque financing structures raised questions about returns, and the Fed's staff flagged the risk in the October minutes.
  • The IPO test: the documented mechanism is the debut, in which Anthropic's record $81.3 billion offering rose 17 percent on Thursday and fell on Friday, showing the listing did not restore confidence in AI stocks.
  • The streak: the documented mechanism is the seven-day decline, in which the S&P 500's longest losing streak since February 2020 put the index in correction territory.
  • Structural note: a correction is a drop of 10 percent or more from a recent high; it is a market label, not a recession signal.

6. Mexico captures 'El Chapo Isidro' in a raid that left 11 dead

What happened: Mexican military forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro," in the city of Guasave in Sinaloa state on Friday night, November 20. Meza Flores is one of the FBI's 10 most wanted fugitives. The firefight between Mexican forces and cartel gunmen left at least 11 people dead: seven alleged cartel gunmen and four Mexican soldiers, according to Mexico's Defense Ministry. Eight people were detained in total, including Meza Flores and his second-in-command, and Meza Flores was flown to Mexico City on Saturday. Mexico's government said in a report on Saturday that intelligence from the Trump administration helped authorities capture him. Meza Flores led the Meza-Flores Transnational Criminal Organization, which controls smuggling routes along the US-Mexico border. On Monday, November 23, Mexico's Security Minister Omar Garcia Harfuch said a US extradition order exists for Meza Flores.

Incentives:

  • The list: the documented mechanism is the FBI's Ten Most Wanted list, in which a fugitive's listing brings US federal resources and a reward to the search.
  • The order: the documented mechanism is the extradition order, in which a US order allows Washington to request Meza Flores's transfer to face charges in the United States.
  • The intelligence: the documented mechanism is the sharing channel, in which US intelligence helped Mexican forces locate Meza Flores, according to Mexico's government.
  • The routes: the documented mechanism is the border trade, in which Meza Flores's organization controls smuggling routes along the US-Mexico border, including for fentanyl and other narcotics.
  • Structural note: the capture removes one leader; it does not by itself dismantle the organization.

Sources:

  • Associated Press, November 21 — Meza Flores captured in Guasave, at least 11 dead, seven gunmen and four soldiers, eight detained, US intelligence helped, Meza Flores led the Beltrán Leyva cartel and later his own group
  • Wall Street Journal, November 22 — captured Friday night after a gunbattle in Guasave, four soldiers and seven gunmen killed, operation carried out with US intelligence, group controls smuggling routes for fentanyl and other narcotics
  • Reuters, November 23 — US extradition order exists for Meza Flores, detained with two other leaders in Sinaloa on Friday night
