1. Peltola wins Alaska outright; Democrats take a 56-44 Senate

What happened: The Associated Press called Alaska's Senate race for Democrat Mary Peltola on Tuesday evening, November 17, two weeks after Election Day. The Alaska Division of Elections' summary report, with all 401 precincts reporting, puts Peltola at 144,302 votes (50.12 percent) and Republican Senator Dan Sullivan at 131,844 (45.80 percent), a lead of 12,458 votes. Because Peltola cleared 50 percent of first-choice votes, she wins outright under Alaska's ranked-choice system, and the ranked-choice tabulation scheduled for Wednesday, November 18, is no longer needed. Republican Gerald Heikes took 6,105 votes (2.12 percent), Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired teacher from Petersburg, took 4,760 (1.65 percent), and write-ins took 885, out of 287,896 total votes. Election officials counted 290,003 ballots from 605,433 registered voters, a turnout of 47.90 percent. Peltola becomes the first Alaska Native to serve in the Senate and the first Democrat to win an Alaska Senate seat since Mark Begich in 2008. The win is the ninth Republican-held Senate seat Democrats flipped in the 2026 midterms, giving them a 56-44 majority when the new Congress takes office in January. The state aims to certify the result on November 25. A New York Times investigation published October 28, based on interviews with 12 sources, text messages, and documents, reported that Peltola campaign aides, the campaign's lawyers, and senior aides at the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee helped recruit Daniel J. Sullivan as part of an internal effort called the "ballot composition project." Peltola said she knew nothing about it, and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee said it had no involvement in recruiting Daniel J. Sullivan or any other Republican candidate. The Justice Department sued Alaska on October 23 seeking to block certification if Sullivan lost; U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed the suit, and the department has appealed. A Justice Department criminal investigation into the recruitment allegations remains open, and Alaska Governor Mike Dunleavy ordered a state investigation.

Incentives:

  • The threshold: the documented mechanism is the 50-percent rule, in which a candidate who clears half of first-choice votes wins outright and no ranked-choice rounds run.
  • The margin: the documented mechanism is the count, in which Peltola's lead of 12,458 votes exceeds the 4,760 votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.
  • The majority: the documented mechanism is the seat count, in which the ninth flip gives Democrats 56 of 100 Senate seats in the next Congress.
  • The legal track: the documented mechanism is the appeal, in which the Justice Department's dismissed suit continues through the appeals process while the criminal investigation stays open and certification is set for November 25.
  • Structural note: the recruitment account comes from the New York Times investigation; Peltola and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee deny involvement, and no charge has been filed.

2. Nvidia posts a record $115.8 billion quarter; the board rules out Huang's return as Commerce closes the cloud loophole

What happened: Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, which ended October 25, up 103 percent from a year earlier and 20 percent from the previous quarter, beating the roughly $109 billion Wall Street expected. Data center revenue reached $107.6 billion, up 110 percent from a year earlier. Adjusted earnings per share were $2.68, against expectations of about $2.47, and net income was $65.3 billion, up 105 percent. Gross margin was 74.8 percent. The company guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3 percent, raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to about 75 percent from 70 percent, and said the fourth-quarter outlook assumes no data center compute revenue from China. The release said shipments of the Vera Rubin NVL72 system have begun, with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, and Nebius. Nvidia returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter through buybacks and dividends and will pay a quarterly dividend of $0.25 per share on December 23 to shareholders of record on December 2. The report was the first in the company's history delivered without Jensen Huang, who resigned as president, chief executive, and board member on November 12. Interim chief executive Colette Kress led the earnings call, and board chairman Harvey Jones said on the call that the board unanimously accepted Huang's resignation and does not intend to reinstate him as chief executive. Huang posted on November 17 that he would be honored to return if the board asked. Shares closed Tuesday at $155.46 and rose 4.82 percent in after-hours trading to $162.95. On Monday, November 16, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security released an interim final rule, "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors," that requires a license to ship advanced AI chips to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil, and requires a license to supply advanced chips to any data center, anywhere in the world, that gives companies headquartered in Macau or a Country Group D:5 destination, a list that includes China, more than 12,000,000 aggregate total processing performance of computing power. The rule takes effect when it is published in the Federal Register, scheduled for November 19, with a 60-day comment period and a 90-day transition window for data centers already serving those customers. It also rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule. The Bureau said the rule was written by and at the direction of the Department of the Treasury and the National Security Council.

Incentives:

  • The board: the documented mechanism is the appointment power, in which only the board can name a chief executive; it has said it does not intend to reinstate Huang and is searching for a permanent successor.
  • The rule: the documented mechanism is the license requirement, in which advanced chips bound for the five named countries, or for data centers above the 12,000,000 TPP threshold serving restricted-country companies, now need Commerce approval.
  • The timing: the documented mechanism is the calendar, in which the rule was released the day before Nvidia's first post-Huang earnings report and takes effect on Federal Register publication, scheduled for November 19.
  • The guidance: the documented mechanism is the outlook, in which the fourth-quarter revenue forecast of $130 billion assumes no data center compute revenue from China.
  • Structural note: the rule names no chipmaker; Nvidia does not disclose what it ships to the five countries.

3. Knesset elects Kahana speaker, 66 to 52, handing Eisenkot's bloc control of the floor

What happened: The Knesset elected Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's center-right Yashar party as its speaker on Tuesday, November 17, by 66 votes to 52, after seven hours of debate. Kahana replaces Amir Ohana of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party. The losing candidate was Likud's Ofir Katz. The speaker decides which legislation reaches a vote in the 120-seat parliament, and Eisenkot's bloc has bills ready for Wednesday, November 18, to repeal legislation affecting the attorney general's authority and the composition of the Judicial Selection Committee. The 26th Knesset was sworn in on November 10 after the October 27 general election left Netanyahu's Likud alliance with 53 seats and Eisenkot's bloc with 48, and President Isaac Herzog tasked Eisenkot with forming a government after he secured the backing of 67 members, compared with 46 for Netanyahu. Eisenkot has until December 6 to assemble a coalition and can seek a two-week extension to December 20; if both he and Netanyahu fail, another election could follow. Negotiators from Yashar, Likud, and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad party met for an hour at the Dan Accadia Hotel in Herzliya on Sunday, November 15, to discuss a unity government; the meeting produced no progress. Likud condemned the speaker vote. Negotiations between Eisenkot's bloc and United Torah Judaism were reported on November 15 to be advancing.

Incentives:

  • The chair: the documented mechanism is the speakership, in which control of the chair decides which bills reach a vote while no government has been formed.
  • The deadline: the documented mechanism is the mandate clock, in which Eisenkot has until December 6, with a possible extension to December 20, before another election becomes possible.
  • The bills: the documented mechanism is the Wednesday agenda, in which the bloc plans to advance repeals of legislation on the attorney general's authority and the Judicial Selection Committee.
  • Structural note: the Knesset was sworn in November 10 with no new government; the speaker vote is a procedural step, not the formation of a coalition.

4. Two tankers attacked in the Strait of Hormuz; two crew members missing as oil falls to $92

What happened: A vessel operated by ADNOC Logistics & Services, the shipping arm of Abu Dhabi's state oil company, was attacked while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on the evening of Tuesday, November 17. The company said on Wednesday that one crew member remains missing and 11 were injured, and the rest of the crew was evacuated. ADNOC did not name the vessel or say who attacked it; maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified it as the Liberian-flagged product tanker Navig8 Messi, struck by an unknown projectile about 13 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman. The Panama-flagged liquefied petroleum gas tanker Tara Gas was struck twice in the strait on November 16, and the second strike set off a fire that forced its 25 crew members to abandon ship. Oman's emergency authorities rescued 24 crew members, and one remains missing; India's foreign ministry said 21 of the 24 Indians aboard were brought ashore at Khasab, two were taken to hospital, and one is unaccounted for. The two attacks bring the number of confirmed attacks on commercial vessels in and around the strait to 18 so far in November. No group has claimed responsibility for the Tara Gas attack. The Revolutionary Guards said six tankers struck naval mines in the strait across Sunday and Monday; Reuters said it could not independently verify the claim, and the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations made no reference to mines in its advisories. The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group arrived in the Middle East over the weekend, and United States Central Command confirmed its arrival on Monday. Brent crude traded at $92.49 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate at $84.73 on Wednesday, after the American Petroleum Institute estimated United States crude inventories rose by 5.3 million barrels in the week to November 13, following a 9 million barrel increase the week before. The International Energy Agency said on November 13 that the world oil market will return to a surplus in the fourth quarter of 2026, with supply averaging 105 million barrels per day, 1.7 million barrels per day above forecast demand. OPEC+ meets on November 29 to set production policy.

Incentives:

  • The blacklist: the documented mechanism is the Persian Gulf Strait Authority list, in which the Navig8 Messi has been listed as a "non-compliant" vessel since August.
  • The claims: the documented mechanism is the verification gap, in which Iran's mine claims carry no vessel names and no independent confirmation, while the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations advisories describe unknown projectiles.
  • The price: the documented mechanism is the inventory build, in which a 5.3 million barrel rise in United States crude stocks and the International Energy Agency's fourth-quarter surplus forecast moved prices down while attacks continued.
  • The escort: the documented mechanism is the carrier presence, in which the Theodore Roosevelt strike group arrived in the region as the attacks continued.
  • Structural note: the two missing crew members are one from each vessel; search and rescue operations were still under way when the companies reported.

5. Senate votes 70-27 to close debate on the permitting package; final passage still ahead

What happened: The Senate voted 70 to 27 on Monday, November 16, to invoke cloture on the Capito substitute amendment to H.R. 2347, the vehicle for the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, the permitting reform package known as BAAJA. The vote was roll call 264. Cloture limits debate; adoption of the substitute and passage of the bill require separate votes, and leaders have not announced a time for a final passage vote. The Senate returns to the bill on Wednesday, when post-cloture time expires and votes on the substitute are expected. The bill was introduced on September 30 by Senators Shelley Moore Capito, Mike Lee, Sheldon Whitehouse, and Martin Heinrich, and the sponsors released a 494-page substitute on November 7. The Senate voted 73 to 25 on November 9 to end debate on taking up the vehicle bill, and 62 to 21 on November 10 to take it up. On the cloture vote, Democrats split 20 to 24, Republicans voted 49 to 2, and the two independents split 1 to 1. On Tuesday, the four sponsors filed another set of changes to the bill's title on historic preservation, amendment 7022, which has not been voted on. That title rewrites Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act, which requires federal agencies to consult Tribal Nations before approving projects that could harm historic, cultural, and sacred places. The package would change federal permitting for energy and infrastructure projects and set what data centers pay for transmission service. The Interior Department's Bureau of Land Management had 1,381 applications for permits to drill pending as of November 2, down from 2,299 in October 2025, and approved 339 drilling applications in October. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries said the bill falls short, leaving the House as the open question if the Senate passes it.

Incentives:

  • The vehicle: the documented mechanism is the substitute, in which the permitting package rides on H.R. 2347, a House-passed bill on the tax treatment of damages for sexual abuse, so the House must approve any version the Senate passes.
  • The split: the documented mechanism is the vote count, in which 20 Democrats joined 49 Republicans and one independent to close debate, while 24 Democrats voted no.
  • The backlog: the documented mechanism is the permit queue, in which 1,381 drilling applications were pending at the Bureau of Land Management as of November 2, down from 2,299 a year earlier.
  • The preservation title: the documented mechanism is Section 106, in which the bill's rewrite of tribal consultation rules drew a further amendment from the sponsors on Tuesday, not yet voted on.
  • Structural note: cloture is a procedural vote to limit debate; it enacts no law and approves no project.

6. Ukraine collapses the Kerch Bridge road span; Abu Dhabi talks produce no breakthrough on land

What happened: Ukrainian forces struck the Kerch Strait Bridge on the night of November 17 for the second consecutive day, collapsing the automobile sections and damaging the railway section, according to the Institute for the Study of War. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces used Palianytsia cruise missile-type drones against the road section and Neptune cruise missiles, Ruta missiles, and FP-5 Flamingo cruise missiles against the railway section, and that the strike was the seventh stage of an operation conducted over several weeks. Ukraine's Security Service said its forces destroyed the metal arch that supported the automobile span. The Russian Ministry of Transport suspended rail and road traffic on the bridge and later resumed traffic on one railway track. Russian occupation officials in Crimea confirmed the strike. Ukrainian forces have struck the bridge in October 2022, July 2023, June 2025, and on the nights of November 15 and 16. The Russian Ministry of Defense claimed its forces intercepted 4,305 Ukrainian drones and 16 Flamingo cruise missiles in 48 hours since about noon on November 15. Russian military bloggers criticized Russian air defense capabilities after the strikes. The Institute assessed that the strikes will force Russian forces to rely on the ground line of communication between occupied Kherson Oblast and Crimea, complicating Russian logistics. On the same nights, Russian long-range strikes targeted bridges in Kyiv City and Zaporizhzhia City, and Russia launched ballistic and cruise missiles and 306 drones, including 92 jet-powered drones, against Ukraine on the night of November 16 to 17. Ukrainian forces struck a petrochemical complex and an oil refinery in the Bashkortostan Republic on the night of November 16 to 17, and several Russian drones violated Moldovan airspace, including over the capital, Chisinau, on the evening of November 17. In the talks, the trilateral negotiations in Abu Dhabi between Ukraine, Russia, and the United States continued this week with territory as the central question; Russian negotiators ruled out territorial concessions, and the earlier round produced agreement on a prisoner exchange of more than 1,000 people. Russian officials continue to reject United States proposals for post-war NATO security architecture and European participation in peace negotiations. At the Ramstein-format Contact Group meeting in Brussels, Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby said Ukrainian long-range strikes deep inside Russia are counterproductive and urged European governments to finance more purchases of American weapons for Ukraine; he also said Ukraine has liberated more territory than Russia has captured for two consecutive months.

Incentives:

  • The bridge: the documented mechanism is the supply line, in which the Kerch Strait Bridge carries Russian logistics between Russia and occupied Crimea; its road span is down and one railway track runs.
  • The talks: the documented mechanism is the territory question, in which Russia demands Ukraine surrender the part of the Donetsk region it still holds, and Zelenskyy has said he would put territorial concessions to a national referendum if Ukraine received security guarantees, reconstruction funds, and the right to maintain a strong army.
  • The air defense: the documented mechanism is the suppression campaign, in which Ukrainian strikes on surface-to-air missile launchers, mobile fire groups, and radar systems degraded Russian air defenses around the bridge.
  • The financing: the documented mechanism is the weapons pipeline, in which European governments pay for American weapons sent to Ukraine, and the Pentagon urged them to pay for more.
  • Structural note: the prisoner exchange agreement came from the earlier Abu Dhabi round; the current round has produced no agreement on territory.
