1. Shutdown passes the 35-day record as the Senate fails a 14th funding vote and a judge orders full SNAP payments

What happened: The shutdown that began on October 1 reached its 37th day on November 6, passing the 35-day shutdown of December 22, 2018 to January 25, 2019 and becoming the longest in United States history. On November 4, the Senate failed for the 14th time to invoke cloture on H.R. 5371, the House-passed continuing resolution that would fund the government at current levels through November 21; the vote was 54 to 44, short of the 60 needed. Democrats have conditioned support on extending Affordable Care Act subsidies that expire at the end of December, and Majority Leader John Thune has offered a vote on the subsidies after the government reopens. The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program dispute continued in the courts: the Agriculture Department said on November 3 it would use $4.65 billion in contingency funds for partial November benefits, then issued revised guidance on November 5 cutting maximum allotments by 35 percent rather than 50 percent. On November 5, Judge John J. McConnell Jr. ordered the department to fully fund November benefits, finding the partial-payment plan arbitrary and capricious, with full payment due November 6. The Justice Department appealed immediately, and the First Circuit declined an administrative stay. A full month of SNAP costs about $8.5 billion to $9 billion, and the program serves about 42 million people.

Incentives:

  • The Senate majority: the documented mechanism is the 60-vote cloture threshold, under which 54 votes in favor still leave the funding bill unable to advance.
  • The Democratic caucus: the documented mechanism is conditioning funding support on the Affordable Care Act subsidy extension, with the subsidies expiring at the end of December.
  • The Agriculture Department: the documented mechanism is the funding-source decision, in which the department chose contingency funds alone on November 3 rather than the full monthly cost the Rhode Island court had ordered.
  • The courts: the documented mechanism is the pair of October 31 orders and the November 5 full-payment order, which set dated requirements the department's partial plan is measured against.
  • Structural note: $4.65 billion covers roughly half a month of benefits at the cited monthly cost, so the contingency-only plan establishes partial coverage, not a funded full month.

2. Two Senate races still uncalled as the incoming majorities set their program and Trump points to the veto

What happened: As of Friday morning, the Associated Press had called 249 House seats for Democrats and 156 for Republicans, with 30 races still uncalled; 218 seats make a majority. Alaska's Senate race remained uncalled, with Democrat Mary Peltola at 102,962 first-choice votes and Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan at 102,325, a margin of 637 votes with about 74 percent of ballots counted; ranked-choice tabulation is scheduled for November 18. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith by 2,087 votes, 467,233 to 465,146, in the latest tally on November 6, with about 3,600 mail ballots still outstanding and arriving through November 10; counties must certify by November 13, and Mississippi has no automatic recount. Nebraska's Senate race was called on November 4, with Republican Senator Pete Ricketts defeating independent Dan Osborn, 373,160 votes to 322,646. The Senate stands at 54 Democrats to 44 Republicans with Alaska and Mississippi uncalled. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said his caucus will move in its first 100 hours to restore Affordable Care Act subsidies, reverse Medicaid cuts, and challenge the administration's tariffs, starting with the tariffs on Canada. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats will use the power of the purse to end the war in Iran. President Donald Trump posted an image of a red cap reading "VETO" on the night of the election and said the following day that he has the power of the veto. Overriding a veto requires 290 votes in the House and 67 in the Senate.

Incentives:

  • The incoming House majority: the documented mechanism is chamber organization, in which the majority party elects the speaker and controls committee chairs, hearing schedules, and subpoena authority.
  • The incoming Senate majority: the documented mechanism is chamber organization, in which the majority party sets the floor agenda and organizes committees, including the handling of nominations.
  • The veto: the documented mechanism is the two-thirds override requirement — 290 House votes and 67 Senate votes — which the incoming majorities do not hold.
  • Structural note: race calls are projections based on counted votes and remaining ballots, not certifications; Mississippi's certification deadline is November 13 and Alaska's ranked-choice tabulation is November 18.

3. Herzog to assign Israel's government mandate on November 8 after Eisenkot takes 67 recommendations

What happened: President Isaac Herzog concluded two days of consultations with all Knesset factions on the evening of November 5. Factions representing 67 lawmakers recommended Gadi Eisenkot, chairman of the Yashar party, to form the next government; factions representing 46 recommended Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu; the seven members of United Torah Judaism declined to recommend either candidate. Herzog said he intends to assign the mandate on Sunday, November 8, after holding personal talks with party leaders; the legal deadline is November 11, and the tasked lawmaker then has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension. The new Knesset is scheduled to be sworn in on November 10. The 67 recommendations for Eisenkot come from 48 seats held by four Jewish-led parties, plus 8 from Ra'am and 11 from the Joint List; the Joint List said it will not join a government he leads. On November 6, Yisrael Beitenu leader Avigdor Liberman said he is willing to compromise on the law exempting ultra-Orthodox yeshiva students from military service, opening the way to a coalition with United Torah Judaism; his conditions were ending the Netanyahu era, no additional elections, and no minority government relying on abstention by the Joint List or Ra'am. Adding United Torah Judaism's 7 seats and Shas's 6 to the bloc's 48 reaches 61, the number needed for a majority.

Incentives:

  • The blocs: the documented mechanism is coalition arithmetic, in which 48 plus 7 plus 6 reaches 61, so Eisenkot's bloc needs ultra-Orthodox backing to reach a majority.
  • The president: the documented mechanism is the consultation and mandate process, in which Herzog tasks the lawmaker with the strongest prospects of securing parliamentary support, with recommendations from parties not binding on the president.
  • United Torah Judaism: the documented mechanism is the draft-law dispute, in which the party withheld its recommendation over legislation on yeshiva student exemptions and conditioned future support on resolving it.
  • Structural note: 67 recommendations do not equal a coalition of 61 seats, and the Joint List said it will not join a government Eisenkot leads.

  • TBS News, November 6 — mandate Sunday, November 8, Knesset sworn in November 10
  • Crisis Zone, November 5 — Joint List position, composition of the 67
  • All Israel News, November 6 — Liberman flexibility on the draft law, conditions for a coalition
  • Israel RealTime, November 5 — consultations concluded, Herzog statement

4. Russia's grid campaign continues as United States-mediated talks are set for November 10 and 11 in Abu Dhabi

What happened: Russian strikes on Ukraine's energy infrastructure continued through the week. Overnight into November 5, Russia launched 128 drones, 65 of them jet-powered Shahed-type aircraft, and air defense shot down or suppressed 105; hits were recorded at 18 locations. Overnight into November 6, Russia launched 227 drones, 86 of them jet-powered, and air defense shot down or suppressed 196; hits were recorded at 14 locations, including an energy facility in Bila Tserkva in the Kyiv region. On November 6, Russian attacks caused new power outages in seven regions — Vinnytsia, Dnipropetrovsk, Zhytomyr, Zaporizhzhia, Kyiv, Kharkiv, and Chernihiv — with the Kyiv area worst affected, Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said. The Energy Ministry had said on November 4 that Russian strikes on substations and lines forced the country's nuclear power plants to cut output. The death toll from the November 2 bombing of an aid distribution point in Komyshuvakha in the Zaporizhzhia region rose to 53, the acting regional governor said on November 6. On the diplomatic track, United States Special Envoy Steve Witkoff announced on November 2 that working-level negotiations between Ukraine and Russia will take place in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11, led on the American side by Witkoff and Jared Kushner and on the Russian side by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky; it is the first trilateral round since February, and European governments will not take part. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the dates on November 3 and held a 90-minute call with Witkoff and Kushner on November 4. On November 5, First Lady Melania Trump hosted Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev and Ukrainian officials at the White House to mark a localized ceasefire she brokered in Oleshky, a Russian-occupied city in the Kherson region.

Incentives:

  • The Russian military: the documented mechanism is the strike campaign on energy infrastructure at the start of the residential heating season, in which substations and transmission lines are hit to force nuclear plants to reduce output.
  • The Ukrainian grid operator: the documented mechanism is the emergency-outage system, in which Ukrenergo cancels or maintains cuts region by region as damage and restoration change.
  • The talks: the documented mechanism is the working-level format in Abu Dhabi, in which the two sides negotiate post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, and humanitarian steps without European governments at the table.
  • The ceasefire channel: the documented mechanism is the localized arrangement in Oleshky, brokered by the first lady and marked by a White House meeting of Russian and Ukrainian representatives.

5. Tankers hit in the Strait of Hormuz as Trump says a decision on Iran is coming

What happened: United States forces carried out limited strikes on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz late on November 2, in an operation first reported by the Wall Street Journal and described by United States officials as defensive, after the Revolutionary Guard Corps fired a surface-to-air missile at an American warplane and after the interception of a missile fired from Qeshm at a commercial vessel; no damage or casualty figures have been released. On November 3, the head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, Mohsen Rezaei, rejected the United States offer passed through Qatari negotiators, saying the response to Iran's seven conditions was insufficient; Iran's proposal calls for the United States to end its blockade on Iranian ports, Iran to reopen the strait, a waiver on Iranian oil sales, cancellation of new sanctions, and then talks on the nuclear file. On November 4, Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei said there should not be even the slightest inclination toward submission to the United States. On November 5, Iran reported three more tankers damaged in the strait; the Revolutionary Guard Corps said two tankers had struck naval mines and another had caught fire, while the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations agency reported a vessel struck by two unidentified projectiles late on November 4 and at least five vessels reporting incidents since November 1, against 35 vessels hit in October, the most of any month of the war. The Iranian claims have not been independently confirmed. On November 5, President Donald Trump met his top national security advisers and said a decision on renewed strikes is coming, adding that the United States is locked and loaded. Vice President JD Vance said on November 4 that the war cost Republicans control of Congress. Brent crude settled at $94.62 on November 4, down 8.7 percent, then at $98.29 on November 5, up $3.67, and traded near $100 on November 6.

Incentives:

  • Tehran: the documented mechanism is the strait itself, in which Iran ties reopening the waterway to an end to the port blockade, a waiver on oil sales, and cancellation of new sanctions.
  • Washington: the documented mechanism is the decision among three paths — renewed large-scale strikes, continued economic pressure, or a negotiated settlement — with the president saying a decision is coming.
  • The oil market: the documented mechanism is the price response, in which Brent fell on hopes the war would ease after the midterm elections and rose on the tanker incidents.
  • Structural note: the tanker incidents are Iranian claims reported by Reuters and other outlets, not independently confirmed; no vessel names, flags, operators, or casualties were confirmed in the day's reporting.

Sources:

  • Reuters, November 5 — tanker incidents, Rezaei rejection, Khamenei, oil down more than $20 on the week
  • Reuters via London South East, November 5 — Brent and WTI settlements
  • Political.org, November 5 — Trump decision meeting, locked and loaded
  • Daily Caller, November 3 — Qeshm strikes, defensive description, Wall Street Journal first report
  • Reuters, November 5 — Vance post, Hegseth defense of the war

6. Markets give back the post-election rally as the October jobs report beats expectations

What happened: On November 4, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1,569.40 points, or 3.03 percent, to close at 53,356.12, with the S&P 500 up 1.58 percent to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.44 percent to 25,736.51. On November 5, the rally reversed: the Dow fell 1,833.06 points, or 3.44 percent, to 51,523.06; the S&P 500 fell 4.36 percent to 7,353.73; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52 percent to 24,315.00, entering correction territory at 11.9 percent below its October 6 record close. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell about 12 percent, and roughly $2 trillion came off Nasdaq market value. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.82 percent, its lowest since September. Reuters attributed the selloff to a reassessment of the artificial intelligence data-center buildout after the Democratic midterm sweep and the state and local data-center limits voters approved on November 3. On November 6, the selloff spread to Asia: the Nikkei 225 fell 6.0 percent to 57,434.77, the Kospi fell 6.0 percent on the close and 12 percent on the week after an intraday trading halt, and the Taiex fell 4.5 percent. The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the October jobs report on November 6: nonfarm payrolls rose 165,000 against about 65,000 expected in the Reuters poll; the unemployment rate fell to 4.1 percent from 4.2 percent; September was revised up to 60,000 and August to 186,000; average hourly earnings rose 0.4 percent to $38.00, up 3.1 percent from a year earlier. The federal funds target stands at 3.75 to 4.00 percent, held at the October 28 meeting of the Federal Open Market Committee, which meets next on December 8 and 9.

Incentives:

  • The data-center buildout: the documented mechanism is the reassessment, in which the Democratic sweep and the local limits approved on November 3 changed the expected pace of approvals and capital spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure.
  • The jobs report: the documented mechanism is the data itself, in which payrolls, wages, and revisions feed into the Federal Reserve's rate decision at its December 8 and 9 meeting.
  • The bond market: the documented mechanism is the move to safety, in which the 10-year yield fell as stocks sold off.
  • Structural note: the Nasdaq's 11.9 percent decline from its October 6 record close passes the 10 percent threshold that defines a correction.
