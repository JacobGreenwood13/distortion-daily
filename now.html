1. Two Italians killed in Kuwait drone strike as Iran claims tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz

What happened: On Thursday, November 26, two Italian technicians were killed in a drone attack on Kuwaiti military installations that Italy and Kuwait attributed to Iran. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani told the Senate in Rome that several Iranian drones targeted Kuwaiti military installations, including an air-defence detection station where the two men worked. The victims were Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, civilian technicians employed by Rheinmetall Italia, the Italian unit of German defence group Rheinmetall, performing maintenance on detection antennas for the Skyguard air-defence system used by Kuwait's armed forces. Kuwait's Defence Ministry said falling debris from the interception of the drones struck a military facility, initially wounding four people. The two Italians later died of their injuries, the ministry confirmed; a Kuwaiti national and a Pakistani national were also wounded. Tehran denied involvement, and Mohammad Marandi, a former Iranian nuclear adviser, claimed the strike was a Ukrainian false-flag operation staged to get President Donald Trump's attention. The attack followed a November 21 Iranian attack on Bahrain that hit a civilian industrial facility and injured two people, and Iranian drone attacks on US positions in Bahrain and Kuwait. The same day, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers had exploded and caught fire after hitting sea mines north of Oman's Khasab port while crossing the Strait of Hormuz through what Tehran called an unauthorised route; one vessel was a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude oil, according to the Guards' statement carried by the Tasnim news agency. Brent crude futures traded near $96 a barrel on Friday, November 27, after gaining nearly $2 on Thursday to settle at their highest since November 6.

Incentives:

  • The attribution: the documented mechanism is the blame exchange, in which Italy and Kuwait attributed the strike to Iranian drones while Tehran denied involvement and a former Iranian adviser claimed a Ukrainian false flag.
  • The target: the documented mechanism is the air-defence station, in which the two technicians were maintaining detection antennas for Kuwait's Skyguard system when the site was hit.
  • The mines: the documented mechanism is the blockade-and-mines exchange, in which US forces stop Iranian crude exports while Iran mines the waterway and demands the blockade end before shipping returns to normal.
  • The price: the documented mechanism is the oil market, in which Brent crude rose to near $96 a barrel as attacks on tankers and Saudi energy infrastructure raised supply concerns.
  • Structural note: the Guards' account of the mine strikes was not independently confirmed; the vessels were not named and no casualty details were given.

2. Moscow talks end without a deal as Trump asks Putin to pull back military aggression across Europe

What happened: US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on November 24, following preliminary consultations with Ukrainian and European officials, and left without an agreement. Kremlin officials said the US-backed draft peace plan contained points that remained unacceptable, and the talks produced no compromise on territory, security guarantees or Europe's role in the process. On November 26, Kushner told the MD Meets podcast that the talks had reached "a little bit of a deadlock" over the fate of Ukrainian territory, and said Putin had agreed not to use nuclear weapons and had reassured the mediators that was not his intention. Kushner said he believed Putin wanted peace, adding that it was "always peace on what terms." On November 26, President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social that he had asked Putin during their meeting in Shenzhen, China, on November 19 to "pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe" in the hope that a deal between Russia and Ukraine could be made quickly, and wrote that thousands of people are being killed each month, calling the war "A COMPLETE BLOODBATH." Trump met NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte at Mar-a-Lago on November 25, and the two issued a joint statement urging Russia to avoid actions that could expand the war. On November 26, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky warned that Russia was preparing another major attack on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, logistics and critical services. Russian missile and drone attacks from November 22 to 25 killed roughly 15 people in Kyiv region and at least nine nationwide in a single 24-hour period, injuring 59, and hit energy infrastructure across Odesa, Zaporizhzhia, Dnipropetrovsk and Poltava, halting output at a DTEK thermal plant. Trump told reporters on November 26 that Putin could attend the G20 summit in Florida on December 14-15 if he wanted, and doubted he would want to; the Kremlin said no decision had been made.

Incentives:

  • The deadlock: the documented mechanism is the territory dispute, in which Moscow demands Ukraine surrender the Donbas and Kyiv rejects ceding territory it still holds, leaving no agreed formula.
  • The assurance: the documented mechanism is the nuclear statement, in which Kushner reported Putin agreed not to use nuclear weapons, while no agreement was reached on territory or a ceasefire.
  • The post: the documented mechanism is the Truth Social message, in which Trump said he asked Putin in Shenzhen to pull back military aggression across Europe, without specifying which territories he meant.
  • The grid: the documented mechanism is the energy campaign, in which Russian strikes on Ukrainian power infrastructure continued through the talks and Zelensky warned of another major attack.
  • The calendar: the documented mechanism is the January target, in which the envoys have pressed for a peace accord as soon as possible, ideally by January, while Trump said he does not bind himself to specific deadlines.
  • Structural note: the Kremlin said no decision had been made on Putin attending the December 14-15 G20 summit in Florida.

3. Houthis widen strikes into Saudi Arabia, hitting refineries, Red Sea ports and Riyadh's financial district

What happened: Yemen's Houthis widened their attacks on Saudi Arabia this week, striking oil infrastructure, Red Sea ports and the capital. On Wednesday, November 25, the Houthis said they had attacked Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery, with a capacity of 550,000 barrels a day, alongside one of the world's largest crude export terminals; satellite images showed thick black smoke over the installation, and a source with knowledge of the matter confirmed an attack on the refinery. The Saudi-led coalition said air defences had intercepted and destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura and did not say the refinery had been struck. On Tuesday, November 24, a Houthi drone struck a tower in Riyadh's financial district for the second time in a week, wounding 11 people; the first strike, on November 19, damaged the building but caused no injuries. The tower houses the headquarters of the Public Investment Fund, the kingdom's trillion-dollar sovereign wealth fund. On Wednesday, a projectile fell at Jazan port on the Red Sea, causing minor damage and no civilian injuries, and a drone was intercepted near Jeddah Islamic Port, Saudi Arabia's biggest port. On Thursday, November 26, a projectile struck an oil refinery in the southwestern Jazan region, killing a Nepalese worker and injuring several others, sparking a fire and temporarily suspending some operations, according to the Saudi Ministry of Energy. The coalition said on Thursday it had intercepted two ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh, two drones over the Eastern Province, a cruise missile launched towards the western region and a drone near Jeddah's port. The Houthis said the port attacks were retaliation for coalition strikes on the port of Hodeidah in Yemen. The attacks follow the reigniting of Yemen's civil war in July, in a spillover from the US-Iran war; Riyadh's international airport was hit several times last month, killing more than a dozen people.

Incentives:

  • The refineries: the documented mechanism is the oil target, in which the Houthis claimed a strike on Ras Tanura and hit the Jazan refinery, while the coalition reported intercepting drones over both areas.
  • The ports: the documented mechanism is the retaliation claim, in which the Houthis said the Jeddah and Jazan port attacks were retaliation for coalition strikes on Hodeidah, the Yemeni port that handles about 90 percent of the country's imported goods.
  • The capital: the documented mechanism is the financial-district campaign, in which Riyadh's financial district has come under almost daily Houthi attacks since early last month, with dozens of people killed.
  • The price: the documented mechanism is the oil market, in which Brent crude traded near $96 a barrel on Friday as attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure and tankers in the Strait of Hormuz raised supply concerns.
  • Structural note: Saudi Arabia has not confirmed whether the Ras Tanura refinery was struck; the coalition said four drones were intercepted over the area.

4. Senate Republicans signal openness to trading Pentagon money for limits on Iran war funding

What happened: Senate Republican leaders signaled openness to a deal with Democrats that would fund the Pentagon while cutting off money for offensive operations in the war with Iran, the Washington Post reported on November 23. Senate Appropriations Committee Chair Susan Collins told reporters she is exploring language that would provide fresh cash to restock depleted munitions while denying the administration funds specifically for continuing the Iran conflict. Senate Majority Leader John Thune told the Post he is "open to anything that keeps the government funded and makes sure that we've got our military and national security requirements and needs met." Senator Tim Kaine said on November 20 that he and a group of Senate Democrats had finalized a proposal to block money for offensive actions against Iran unless President Donald Trump seeks and receives a formal war authorization from Congress, while permitting spending to defend US troops and evacuate US civilians. Senator Patty Murray, the leading Democrat on the Appropriations Committee, confirmed the funding limits are under active discussion between the parties. Senator Josh Hawley said he could support the framework. Senator Tammy Duckworth, who with Senator Elizabeth Warren drafted an amendment to cut off money for offensive operations while paying for defense, intelligence and evacuations, said on November 19 that she would vote against any funding bill that does not cut off spending on the war. Deputy Defense Secretary Steve Feinberg appeared before Senate Republicans in a closed-door session to press the case for additional military appropriations. The administration is seeking $350 billion in extra defense funding. The Senate rejected a war powers resolution on the conflict on November 19 by 48 votes to 51. The federal government is funded through December 11 under the stopgap law P.L. 119-103, and any spending bill needs 60 Senate votes; Republicans hold 53 seats until January 3, when Democrats take control with a 56-seat majority. Congress is in Thanksgiving recess and returns the week of November 30. Trump told the New York Post in an interview published November 24 that the war effort is funded through the end of January, and warned that Democrats could turn what he called a "total win" into "a loss" if they stop the war.

Incentives:

  • The trade: the documented mechanism is the stopgap bargain, in which Democrats approve money for the Pentagon and in return the stopgap restricts what the administration can spend on the war.
  • The votes: the documented mechanism is the 60-vote threshold, in which any spending bill needs 60 Senate votes, so Republicans need at least seven Democrats while Democrats need Republican votes for their limits.
  • The clock: the documented mechanism is the December 11 deadline, in which funding runs out and Congress returns from recess with less than two weeks to reach a deal.
  • The carve-outs: the documented mechanism is the Kaine proposal, in which money for defending US troops and evacuating civilians stays available while offensive operations lose funding unless Congress authorizes the war.
  • The exit: the documented mechanism is the authorization clause, in which the limit would lift if Congress authorizes the war, and the restriction lasts only as long as the stopgap.
  • Structural note: it is unclear how many Senate Republicans would support the deal, and the House has not taken it up; Democrats favor a stopgap extending into early 2027, when they take control.

5. Alaska certifies Peltola's Senate win as Sullivan concedes, locking a 56-seat Democratic majority

What happened: Alaska's Division of Elections certified the November 3 general election results on Monday, November 23, two days ahead of schedule. Democrat Mary Peltola won the US Senate race outright with 144,583 votes, or 50.14 percent, defeating Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan, who received 132,022 votes, or 45.78 percent, a margin of 12,561 votes. Republican Gerald Heikes received 6,107 votes, or 2.12 percent; Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired educator from Petersburg who shared the incumbent's name, received 4,767 votes, or 1.65 percent; write-in candidates received 886 votes. All 401 precincts reported, with 290,483 voters casting ballots out of 605,433 registered, a turnout of 47.98 percent. Because Peltola won an outright majority of first-choice votes, no ranked-choice tabulation was needed. Sullivan conceded on Tuesday, November 24, saying he accepted the results certified by the Division of Elections out of respect for the people of Alaska and the democratic process. Peltola, who served as Alaska's at-large US Representative from 2022 to 2025, will take office when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027, and will be the first Alaska Native in the Senate and the first Democrat to win an Alaska Senate seat since 2008. The certification followed a failed attempt by President Donald Trump to block certification if anyone other than Sullivan won; the Justice Department dropped its civil bid after a federal judge dismissed it, while criminal subpoenas and a House Oversight probe remain active. Democrats will hold a 56-44 majority in the Senate in the 120th Congress. Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins won the governor's race with 51.09 percent of first-choice votes, and Republican incumbent Nick Begich won reelection to Alaska's US House seat with 51.4 percent after ranked-choice tabulation. The deadline to request a recount is November 28 for most statewide races, and the deadline to file an election contest in Alaska Superior Court is December 3.

Incentives:

  • The majority: the documented mechanism is the January 3 convening, in which Democrats take a 56-44 Senate majority, changing who writes the next funding bills.
  • The name: the documented mechanism is the ballot, in which a namesake candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., drew 4,767 votes, or 1.65 percent, which was not enough to affect the outcome.
  • The challenge: the documented mechanism is the certification fight, in which the Justice Department dropped its civil bid to block certification after a federal judge dismissed it, while criminal subpoenas and a House Oversight probe remain active.
  • The calendar: the documented mechanism is the contest window, in which recount requests close November 28 and election contests must be filed by December 3.
  • Structural note: Peltola won an outright majority, so no ranked-choice rounds were needed, and she received more votes than the other three candidates combined.

6. France closes the 2026 books at a 5.4 percent deficit as the Senate kills crypto tax measures

What happened: France's Parliament definitively adopted the end-of-year budget adjustment law for 2026 on Tuesday, November 24, completing the final budget adjustments of the year and keeping the public deficit target at 5.4 percent of gross domestic product. The Senate approved the text by 231 votes to 111 and the National Assembly by 135 votes to 64, with 89 deputies abstaining, after a joint committee of both chambers agreed a common version on November 20. The law opens 700 million euros in credits for the armed forces and 223 million euros in support for farmers, road hauliers and fishermen, and funds drought relief and Middle East war costs. The 5.4 percent target exceeds the 5 percent goal the government set in January; Public Accounts Minister David Amiel praised the compromise, while Senate rapporteur Christine Lavarde said the text recorded the failure of the 2026 budget execution. The European Commission gave its approval to France's fiscal trajectory on November 24 while maintaining reservations about the sustainability of the debt over the medium term; the Commission's own forecast puts the 2026 deficit at 5.1 percent of GDP and public debt rising to about 120 percent of GDP by 2027. S&P is scheduled to review France's sovereign rating on Friday, November 27, currently holding the country at A+ with a stable outlook. The 2027 budget remains unsettled: the National Assembly unanimously rejected the revenue section of the bill on November 4 by 458 votes to none, and the Senate has been examining the government's original text since November 20, with a final vote set for December 9. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu told senators on November 25 that he had no intention of forcing the budget through and called for a compromise, while holding the 5 percent deficit target as non-negotiable. On November 24, the Senate also rejected crypto tax measures the Assembly had approved, including a disclosure requirement for self-custodied holdings above 100,000 euros, a tax on stablecoin conversions and an extension of the crypto exit tax.

Incentives:

  • The miss: the documented mechanism is the deficit target, in which the 2026 deficit stands at 5.4 percent of GDP against the 5 percent goal set in January, and the European Commission's forecast is 5.1 percent.
  • The rating: the documented mechanism is the S&P review, in which the agency's scheduled November 27 assessment follows the adoption of the adjustment law, with the country rated A+ with a stable outlook.
  • The fallback: the documented mechanism is the 2027 calendar, in which the Senate's final vote falls on December 9 and the government retains Article 49.3 or an ordinance as a fallback if no compromise is reached.
  • The crypto vote: the documented mechanism is the Senate rejection, in which the upper chamber voted down disclosure, stablecoin conversion and exit-tax measures the Assembly had approved, leaving them out of the 2026 books.
  • Structural note: the Assembly's 135-64 vote came with 89 abstentions, so the text passed with 135 deputies voting in favor out of 577 seats.
