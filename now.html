1. Two tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz as Iran's attacks on Bahrain and Kuwait continue

What happened: On Wednesday, November 25, two oil tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, exploded and caught fire, according to a statement from Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy carried by state media. The statement said the vessels had been attempting to transit an unauthorised route through the waterway. An Iranian Army spokesperson said the military had deployed new systems and upgraded existing ones to strengthen surveillance and control of the strait, and warned that vessels passing through without coordinating with Iranian forces had faced greater risks. Tehran told Washington it will not restore normal shipping through the strait until its demands are met, including the lifting of the US naval blockade of Iranian ports and an end to the war on all fronts; through Qatari mediators, Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions. The mine strikes followed a November 21 Iranian attack on Bahrain that hit a civilian industrial facility and injured two people, and Iranian drone attacks on US positions in Bahrain and Kuwait in recent days. On November 24, the Revolutionary Guards claimed they struck two US Patriot radar systems in Bahrain and downed two American drones over the strait; those claims were not independently verified. US Central Command says its maritime blockade has prevented Iran from loading any crude oil onto its tankers since late August. On November 20, Vice President JD Vance declined at a White House press conference to say whether he would have ordered the February strikes on Iran, saying the decision was President Donald Trump's call and that his job is to support the president; Trump said he was not unhappy with Vance's answer but said it lacked an acknowledgment that the strikes were necessary and that Vance would make the same decision. Vance had written in a post-election postmortem that the Iran war was the reason Republicans lost the midterm elections. On November 24, Trump told the New York Post he is inclined to order renewed military strikes after the New Year if negotiations fail, and called the Iranian side "professional tap dancers" for talking without making progress. On November 23, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Qatar's prime minister held the eighth US-Qatar Strategic Dialogue in Washington, agreeing to deepen cooperation on defence, security, trade and investment.

Incentives:

  • The blockade: the documented mechanism is the naval blockade, in which US Central Command says it has prevented Iran from loading any crude oil onto its tankers since late August, cutting Tehran's oil revenue.
  • The mines: the documented mechanism is the blockade-and-mines exchange, in which US forces stop Iranian crude exports while Iran mines the waterway and demands the blockade end before shipping returns to normal.
  • The 48-hour offer: the documented mechanism is the Qatari channel, in which Tehran conveyed through Qatari mediators that it would reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions.
  • The split: the documented mechanism is the vice president's postmortem, in which Vance tied the Iran war to the Republicans' midterm losses and declined at the White House to say he would have ordered the strikes.
  • The deadline: the documented mechanism is the January calendar, in which Trump said he is inclined to order renewed strikes after the New Year if negotiations fail, while federal funding runs out on December 11 and the new Congress takes office on January 3, 2027.
  • Structural note: the Guards' claims of hits on US equipment were not independently verified; Bahrain confirmed only the industrial facility fire and the two injuries.

2. Zelensky turns to the new Congress as the US shelves business deals for Russia and Patriot missiles sit unapproved

What happened: Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said in a Bloomberg interview published on November 24 that he will reach out to the new Democrat-controlled US Congress to intensify pressure on Russia if President Donald Trump fails to convince President Vladimir Putin to end the war by the end of the year. Ukraine will seek a large package of US military aid and tougher sanctions on Russia if no peace deal acceptable to Kyiv is reached. US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff are pressing for a peace accord as soon as possible, ideally by January, and have floated proposals for Ukraine to cede the Luhansk and Donetsk regions, collectively known as the Donbas, to Russia in exchange for some form of security guarantees from NATO. On November 25, the Financial Times reported that the Trump administration has quietly shelved proposals to use business deals as an incentive for Russia to end the war, after the Kremlin showed little interest in the proposed energy and aviation ventures; Kushner and Witkoff told European officials that corporate tie-ups were no longer a core aspect of the US pitch. A US official described the report as "not accurate" and said the administration is organising its negotiating tracks and that business deals continue to be an element. The proposals had included US involvement in the Nord Stream pipelines, investment in Russian oil projects and titanium production, and the resumption of energy supplies to Europe. On November 24, the Financial Times reported that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has yet to approve the transfer of about 60 Patriot air defence interceptors already allocated to Ukraine, including PAC-3 interceptors, the newest Patriot missile variant, scheduled for delivery between September and December, and about 20 older PAC-2 missiles; none of the scheduled shipments has been approved. A Pentagon spokesperson said there had been no change in US policy regarding support for Ukraine. In an interview with the New York Post published on November 24, Trump laughed when told his negotiators expect peace by January, asked who said that, and said he does not bind himself to specific deadlines. Kushner and Witkoff arrived in Moscow on November 24 for talks with Putin after preliminary consultations with Ukrainian and European officials. Zelensky said on November 23 that Ukraine is ready to join four-way talks with Europe, the United States and Russia as soon as possible, with partners aiming to hold the meeting before Christmas.

Incentives:

  • The Congress channel: the documented mechanism is the January 3 convening, in which the new Democratic Congress takes office and becomes the audience for Kyiv's request for a military aid package and tougher sanctions if the talks fail.
  • The dropped lever: the documented mechanism is the business-deal pitch, in which the US offered energy and aviation ventures to Moscow as an incentive to end the war, and shelved the offer after the Kremlin showed little interest.
  • The missiles: the documented mechanism is the transfer approval, in which US-made Patriot interceptors held in European arsenals cannot move to Ukraine without Pentagon approval, and about 60 remain unapproved while Russian strikes on Ukrainian cities continue.
  • The guarantee: the documented mechanism is the Article 5-style proposal, in which a collective-defence commitment would bind the United States and its allies to respond to a renewed Russian attack; Moscow has rejected Ukrainian NATO membership in any form.
  • Structural note: the Financial Times report on the shelved business deals was described as "not accurate" by a US official, and Reuters said it could not immediately verify the Patriot report; the Pentagon said its policy on support for Ukraine had not changed.

  • Reuters, November 25 — Financial Times report: business-deal proposals shelved after the Kremlin showed little interest; US official called the report "not accurate"
  • Reuters, November 24 — Financial Times report: Hegseth has yet to approve about 60 Patriot interceptors; Pentagon said no change in policy

3. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang and the market posts its best day since April 2025

What happened: Nvidia announced on Monday, November 23, that its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and chief executive, effective immediately, 11 days after he stepped down on November 11. The board had said his departure followed personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct; reporting tied the resignation to two extramarital affairs, including one with a woman US officials believed was working with Chinese intelligence. Nvidia shares rose 31.48 percent on Monday to close at $165.05, their best day since 2003, adding $952 billion in market value. The shares had closed at $126 on Friday, November 20, down 48 percent from their October 6 peak of about $240, wiping out roughly $2.8 trillion in market value in 45 days. Even after Monday's rebound, the stock remained about 32 percent below its early-October peak and 5 percent below where it stood before Huang stepped down. The S&P 500 rose 4.73 percent on Monday, the Nasdaq Composite rose 6.51 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1,448 points, or 3.01 percent. As of Monday's close, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq were down 7.4 percent and 12.7 percent, respectively, from their October 6 peaks. Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103 percent from a year earlier. Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress in early December about the espionage case and the question of whether US restrictions on sales of advanced chips to China were circumvented; the board is still investigating whether he was the target of a foreign spy and whether any national-security concerns were created. The rally faded on Tuesday, and by Wednesday AI stocks were selling off again after hot inflation data.

Incentives:

  • The board: the documented mechanism is the reinstatement vote, in which the board unanimously reversed its position less than a week after ruling out Huang's return, removing the leadership uncertainty that had driven the sell-off.
  • The testimony: the documented mechanism is the December 3 Senate appearance, in which Huang is scheduled to testify before Congress about the espionage case and the chip-sale restrictions.
  • The valuation: the documented mechanism is the revenue report, in which Nvidia's fiscal third-quarter revenue rose 103 percent year over year to $115.8 billion, while the stock still closed Monday about 32 percent below its early-October peak.
  • The streak: the documented mechanism is the seven-day decline that preceded the rebound, in which the S&P 500's longest losing streak since February 2020 had put the index in correction territory before Monday's rally.
  • Structural note: Monday's gains left the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq still down 7.4 percent and 12.7 percent from their October 6 peaks; the rally faded on Tuesday.

  • Morningstar, November 23 — reinstatement 11 days after resignation following the revelation of two extramarital affairs, including with a woman US officials believed worked with Chinese intelligence

4. Third-quarter growth revised up to 4.3 percent as October core inflation hits 3.3 percent

What happened: The Bureau of Economic Analysis reported on Wednesday, November 25, that real gross domestic product increased at an annual rate of 4.3 percent in the third quarter of 2026, revised up 0.9 percentage point from the 3.4 percent advance estimate, primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending. Consumer spending rose 4.2 percent, revised up from 3.8 percent. The price index for gross domestic purchases increased 2.7 percent, revised down from 3.0 percent. In the same morning's personal income and outlays report, the core personal consumption expenditures price index — the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge, which excludes food and energy — rose 0.3 percent in October from September and 3.3 percent from a year earlier, above the 3.2 percent economists expected and the highest 12-month reading since November 2023. Headline PCE rose 0.3 percent on the month and 3.8 percent on the year, in line with forecasts. Initial jobless claims fell to 189,000, below the 200,000 expected. Durable-goods orders rose 0.2 percent in October, below the 0.5 percent expected. Markets priced about a 98 percent chance of a quarter-point rate increase at the Federal Reserve's December 8-9 meeting, according to CME FedWatch, and S&P 500 futures fell 2.1 percent after the data. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent warned in a November 22 television interview that the Fed risked damaging the economy by raising interest rates too far; the minutes of the October 27-28 Fed meeting showed broad support for further hikes, with Governor Christopher Waller arguing for considering a 50-basis-point move in December. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point.

Incentives:

  • The rate path: the documented mechanism is the futures market, in which CME FedWatch pricing near 98 percent reflects traders' expectation of a quarter-point increase at the December 8-9 meeting.
  • The split: the documented mechanism is the Treasury-Fed divide, in which the Treasury Secretary publicly warned against raising rates too far while the Fed's October minutes showed broad support for further hikes.
  • The revision: the documented mechanism is the second estimate, in which the Bureau of Economic Analysis revised third-quarter growth up by 0.9 percentage point on stronger investment and consumer spending, while revising the purchases price index down to 2.7 percent.
  • The labour market: the documented mechanism is the claims count, in which initial jobless claims fell to 189,000, below the 200,000 expected, alongside growth of 4.3 percent.
  • Structural note: the 3.3 percent core PCE reading is the highest since November 2023; the next GDP estimate is due December 23.

  • CNN, November 25 — economy grew 4.3 percent, up from 3.4 percent initially reported; PCE accelerated to 3.8 percent in October

5. Consumer confidence jumps to 103.3, the highest since January 2025

What happened: The Conference Board said on November 24 that its Consumer Confidence Index rose 11.4 points to 103.3 in November, up from an upwardly revised 91.9 in October, the highest level since January 2025. The Present Situation Index, based on consumers' assessment of current business and labor market conditions, increased 13.7 points to 132.8. The Expectations Index, based on the short-term outlook for income, business and labor-market conditions, improved 9.9 points to 83.6. The survey was conducted November 1-17. The reading topped every estimate in a Bloomberg survey of economists, whose highest individual forecast was 94.7. Fergus McCormick, a senior economist at the Conference Board, said the rally was potentially due to lower uncertainty following the midterm elections. The November reading contrasted with the University of Michigan's consumer sentiment gauge, which remains weak.

Incentives:

  • The survey: the documented mechanism is the Conference Board's monthly survey, conducted November 1-17 on an online sample, in which the Present Situation Index rose 13.7 points and the Expectations Index rose 9.9 points.
  • The election: the documented mechanism is the calendar, in which the survey period covered the November 3 midterm elections, and the Conference Board's senior economist said the rally was potentially due to lower uncertainty after the vote.
  • The split: the documented mechanism is the second gauge, in which the University of Michigan's consumer sentiment measure remains weak even as the Conference Board's index jumped.
  • Structural note: the Expectations Index stood at 83.6, below the Present Situation Index of 132.8; the cutoff date for the preliminary results was November 17.

6. Federal funding runs out in 16 days with none of the 12 spending bills enacted

What happened: The federal government is funded through Friday, December 11, 2026, under the stopgap law P.L. 119-103, signed on September 2, 2026. As of November 25, none of the 12 regular fiscal 2027 spending bills is law: the House has passed three and the Senate has passed none, and Congress.gov listed no fiscal 2027 bill reported by the Senate Appropriations Committee. A funding gap would begin on December 12 if Congress has not enacted the regular bills or another stopgap by the end of December 11. The House and Senate are in recess for Thanksgiving and return the week of November 30. Senate Appropriations Committee Republicans released the final five appropriations bills last week as a starting point for negotiations: State-Foreign Operations, Defense, Financial Services-General Government, Labor-HHS-Education, and Transportation-HUD. The administration is considering a stopgap running through March, while the top House Appropriations Democrat wants it as short as possible; the most likely outcome, according to fiscal reporting, is a stopgap into early 2027, possibly with one or more of the less controversial full-year bills attached, such as Military Construction-Veterans Affairs. The administration's proposed $1.5 trillion defense funding package faces uncertain prospects; Senate appropriators recommended approximately $1.1 trillion in defense spending in their version of the fiscal 2027 defense appropriations bill, released November 16. The $350 billion defense reconciliation measure is dead, though the House Armed Services Committee chair said there is a path for a scaled-down version. The Government Accountability Office identified $9 billion in unappropriated funds drawn from the Treasury in violation of the Constitution's Appropriations Clause.

Incentives:

  • The votes: the documented mechanism is the Senate's 60-vote threshold, in which 60 votes are required to end debate and pass appropriations bills, so avoiding a shutdown on December 12 requires bipartisan agreement.
  • The lame duck: the documented mechanism is the calendar, in which the outgoing Republican Congress holds both chambers until January 3, 2027, when Democrats take control with a 56-44 Senate majority and at least 258 House seats.
  • The defense gap: the documented mechanism is the appropriations split, in which the administration proposed $1.5 trillion in defense funding while Senate appropriators recommended about $1.1 trillion, and the $350 billion reconciliation measure is dead.
  • The watchdog: the documented mechanism is the Appropriations Clause, in which the Government Accountability Office identified $9 billion in unappropriated funds drawn from the Treasury.
  • Structural note: in all six midterm lame ducks since 2002, the first deadline after the election was extended.
