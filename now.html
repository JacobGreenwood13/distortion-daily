1. Democrats win control of the House and the Senate in the November 3 midterm elections

What happened: Voters cast ballots on November 3 for all 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 seats in the Senate. The Associated Press called the Senate for the Democrats late on November 3 and the House for the Democrats early on November 4. Senate seats flipped from Republican to Democratic control in Texas, Kansas, Iowa, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, and South Carolina, according to Associated Press race calls reported by multiple outlets. With Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled, the Associated Press tracker showed Democrats at 54 Senate seats and Republicans at 44. A majority in the House requires 218 seats. The AP determination of control is not a certified final seat tally, and the final size of each majority remained unsettled while counting continued. The new Congress is scheduled to convene in January 2027. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries is positioned to become speaker, but that office is filled by election in the incoming House, not by the AP call itself.

Incentives:

  • The incoming House majority: the documented mechanism is chamber organization, in which the majority party elects the speaker and controls committee chairs, hearing schedules, and subpoena authority.
  • The incoming Senate majority: the documented mechanism is chamber organization, in which the majority party sets the floor agenda and organizes committees, including the handling of nominations.
  • The president: the documented mechanism is the shift from unified party control of the White House and Congress to divided government, under which legislation requires support from a Democratic majority in at least one chamber.
  • Structural note: race calls are projections based on counted votes and remaining ballots, not certifications; state certification processes continue after the calls.

Sources:

  • Associated Press, U.S. Senate election results, November 3 — 35 Senate races, Alaska and Mississippi uncalled
  • Associated Press, U.S. House election results, November 3 — national House vote totals
  • Financial Channel, November 4 — AP race calls for both chambers, seven flipped Senate seats, Reuters pre-election assessment of the prior 218-seat Republican House margin
  • Cumhuriyet Daily, November 4 — 218-seat House threshold, 25 flipped House districts listed, new Congress convening in January 2027

2. Texas flips both top statewide races: Talarico takes the Senate seat, Hinojosa unseats Abbott

What happened: The Associated Press called the Texas U.S. Senate race for Democrat James Talarico over Republican Ken Paxton, with Talarico at 5,440,114 votes and Paxton at 4,248,109 votes in the AP totals. The seat was open after Senator John Cornyn, whom Paxton defeated in a Republican primary runoff in May, left the race. The Associated Press called the Texas governor race for Democrat Gina Hinojosa over Republican Governor Greg Abbott, with Hinojosa at 5,226,388 votes and Abbott at 4,523,160 votes in the AP totals. Abbott conceded the governor race on November 3. Talarico is the first Democrat to win a U.S. Senate election in Texas since 1988. Hinojosa is the first Democrat elected governor of Texas since Ann Richards in 1990 and the first Latina to hold the office. Both take office in January 2027.

Incentives:

  • The Senate seat: the documented mechanism is Senate arithmetic, in which the Texas flip is one of the seven Republican-held seats that moved the chamber to a Democratic majority.
  • The governor's office: the documented mechanism is party control of the state executive, which changes hands after three terms under Abbott, with the new governor taking office in January 2027.
  • Structural note: the vote totals above are Associated Press totals at the time of the calls, with precincts still reporting in the AP data, so the counts are not final certified totals.

Sources:

  • Associated Press, U.S. Senate, Texas, November 3 — Talarico and Paxton vote totals, race called
  • Associated Press, Governor, Texas, November 3 — Hinojosa and Abbott vote totals, race called
  • USA Today, Texas election recap, November 3 — Abbott concession, Talarico Senate win, Secretary of State vote shares
  • El Paso Times, November 4 — first Democratic Senate win in Texas since 1988, first Democratic governor since 1990, first Latina governor

3. Shutdown reaches day 35 as the SNAP full-payment order and the administration's contingency-only plan collide

What happened: On November 4, the federal government shutdown reached its 35th day, counting from October 1, matching the length of the 35-day shutdown in 2018 and 2019 cited in reporting on November 3. A Senate vote on funding was scheduled for November 4. The Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program dispute from the October 31 court orders remained unresolved in practice: the Rhode Island order had required full November payments by November 3 or a plan for partial benefits by November 5, while the Boston order required a plan for at least partial payments by November 3. On November 3, the Agriculture Department opted to use only contingency funding, providing $4.65 billion to cover benefits, according to Reuters reporting. A full month of SNAP costs about $8.5 billion to $9 billion, and the program serves about 42 million people. The contingency-only position and the full-payment order therefore point to different November benefit levels, with states administering the program while the courts and Congress continue to act.

Incentives:

  • The Agriculture Department: the documented mechanism is the funding-source decision, in which the department chose contingency funds alone on November 3 rather than tapping additional sources the Rhode Island court had identified.
  • The courts: the documented mechanism is the pair of October 31 orders with dated compliance deadlines of November 3 and November 5, which set the legal baseline the department's November 3 position is measured against.
  • Congress: the documented mechanism is the funding lapse itself, which continues until a funding bill passes, and the Senate vote scheduled for November 4.
  • Structural note: $4.65 billion covers roughly half a month of benefits at the cited monthly cost, so the contingency-only plan establishes partial coverage, not a funded full month.

Sources:

  • National Low Income Housing Coalition, October 31 rulings summary — Boston and Rhode Island orders, November 3 and November 5 deadlines, 42 million recipients
  • Produce Market Guide, Reuters report — Agriculture Department November 3 contingency-only choice at $4.65 billion, $8.5 billion to $9 billion monthly cost, Rhode Island full-payment order
  • Nexa News, November 4 — shutdown at day 34 on November 3, one day short of the 35-day 2019 record, Senate funding vote scheduled for November 4

4. Herzog receives certified Israeli results and opens coalition consultations on November 4

What happened: On November 4, President Isaac Herzog received the certified results of Israel's October 27 Knesset election from Central Elections Committee chair Justice Noam Sohlberg and opened consultations on who receives the first mandate to form a government. The certified count gives Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc 53 of the 120 Knesset seats, the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot 48 seats, and Arab-led parties 19 seats. Sixty-one seats are needed for a majority, and no mandate had been announced by the evening of November 4. Herzog is hearing the smaller factions first, with Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar party scheduled late on November 5. On November 3, five opposition leaders met in Herzliya and agreed to recommend Eisenkot to Herzog, and Religious Zionism and Shas said they would recommend Netanyahu. The Central Elections Committee said its review found no evidence of irregularities that compromised the election's integrity or changed its results. Herzog must task a Knesset member with forming a government within seven days of the official results, a deadline of November 11, and the tasked lawmaker then has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension. The new Knesset is scheduled to be sworn in on November 10, and Netanyahu remains in office as caretaker during negotiations.

Incentives:

  • The blocs: the documented mechanism is coalition arithmetic, in which 53 plus 19 reaches 72 and 48 plus 19 reaches 67, so either bloc needs Arab-led party backing to reach 61.
  • The president: the documented mechanism is the consultation and mandate process, in which Herzog tasks the lawmaker with the strongest prospects of securing parliamentary support, with recommendations from parties not binding on the president.
  • The caretaker government: Netanyahu stays in office during negotiations, which keeps executive authority with the incumbent while talks continue.
  • Structural note: recommendations to the president are public positions, not coalition agreements, and no mandate had been issued as of November 4.

Sources:

  • Crisis Zone, November 4 — certified results received November 4, 53-48-19 count, smallest-first consultation order, November 11 mandate deadline
  • Asia Today, November 4 — five opposition leaders in Herzliya on November 3 recommending Eisenkot, 28-day mandate with possible 14-day extension, Knesset sworn in November 10
  • Clacton and Frinton Gazette, Associated Press, November 4 — committee finding of no fraud affecting the outcome, consultations beginning November 4, Netanyahu remaining in office during talks

5. Russia launches its largest energy strike of the year on Ukraine overnight into November 3; nuclear plants cut output on November 4

What happened: Overnight into November 3, Russian forces launched 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles, and 59 cruise missiles against Ukraine, primarily targeting energy infrastructure in nine regions, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Ukraine's grid operator Ukrenergo said energy facilities were hit more than 20 times in the preceding 24 hours and described the attack as one of the largest on energy infrastructure since the full-scale invasion began. DTEK, Ukraine's largest private energy company, said three of its five thermal power plants stopped generating. A Russian strike on a site in southern Ukraine where people had gathered to receive humanitarian aid raised the death toll there to 29 on November 3, with 48 still missing, according to Associated Press reporting. On November 4, Ukraine's Energy Ministry said Russian strikes had forced the country's nuclear power plants to cut output, without naming the plants or the amounts, after strikes on substations and lines. Ukrenergo cancelled emergency outages in Kyiv and most regions on November 4, while the Kharkiv region stayed on emergency cuts and some regions faced rationing of up to 16 hours a day. Russia's Defense Ministry said on November 4 that it had hit two power stations in the Kyiv and Zhytomyr regions, keeping up near-daily strikes on energy facilities as temperatures fall. The strikes fall a week before U.S.-mediated talks were set to resume, according to Reuters reporting.

Incentives:

  • The Russian military: the documented mechanism is the strike campaign on energy infrastructure at the start of the residential heating season, which Kyiv's mayor said began on November 2.
  • The Ukrainian grid operator: the documented mechanism is the emergency-outage system, in which Ukrenergo cancels or maintains cuts region by region as damage and restoration change.
  • The nuclear safety channel: the documented mechanism is the substation link, in which strikes on substations and lines force nuclear plants to reduce output even when no reactor itself is reported hit, a pattern Ukraine raised as a safety issue.
  • Structural note: the ministry did not name the plants affected or state how far output fell, and no strike on a reactor itself has been reported.

Sources:

  • Institute for the Study of War, Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment, November 3 — 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles, 59 cruise missiles, more than 20 energy facility hits, three DTEK plants stopped generating
  • WAMC, Associated Press, November 3 — aid-site death toll of 29 with 48 missing, overnight barrage across nine regions, emergency power cuts
  • Crisis Zone, November 4 — Energy Ministry nuclear output cut, emergency outages cancelled in Kyiv and most regions, Kharkiv continued, rationing up to 16 hours in some regions
  • KRRO, Reuters, November 4 — Russian Defense Ministry claim on Kyiv and Zhytomyr power stations, near-daily energy strikes, U.S.-mediated talks set to resume

6. Alaska's Senate race remains uncalled on November 4, with ranked-choice tabulation set for November 18

What happened: The Associated Press listed Alaska's U.S. Senate race as too early to call on November 4, with Democrat Mary Peltola at 102,701 first-choice votes and Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan at 102,075 first-choice votes in the AP totals, a margin of 626 votes. Independent candidate Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. drew 3,583 first-choice votes, and Republican Gerald Heikes drew 4,821. The two leading candidates traded the lead on election night, and thousands of absentee and early votes remained to be counted, including ballots from rural precincts and mailed ballots that can still arrive. Alaska's Division of Elections scheduled official ranked-choice tabulation for November 18, after all eligible ballots have been reviewed and counted. Under Alaska's system, a candidate who finishes the first-choice count with more than 50 percent wins outright; in races without a first-choice majority, lower finishers are eliminated and their ballots transfer to the next ranked candidate still in contention. The race no longer decides Senate control, after the Democrats secured a Senate majority, but it sets the size of that majority. The Justice Department escalated a criminal investigation into the recruitment of Daniel J. Sullivan on Election Day, sending subpoenas to Democratic campaigners cited in reporting, according to the Juneau Empire. The November 1 dismissal of the Justice Department's ballot-segregation lawsuit remains in effect unless the appeals court acts.

Incentives:

  • The Division of Elections: the documented mechanism is the counting calendar, in which mailed ballots continue to arrive and ranked-choice tabulation is set for November 18, so the published totals move until that count is complete.
  • The candidates: the documented mechanism is ranked-choice redistribution, in which the 3,583 first-choice votes for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. and the 4,821 for Heikes transfer to remaining candidates if no candidate reaches 50 percent on first choices.
  • The Justice Department: the documented mechanism is the criminal investigation and the pending appeal of the November 1 dismissal, which run alongside the count without themselves changing ballot handling unless a court acts.
  • Structural note: the 626-vote margin is a snapshot of counted votes, not a result, and the returns show how many first-choice votes each Sullivan received without showing how many voters intended to choose the other man.

Sources:

  • Associated Press, U.S. Senate, Alaska, November 3 — race too early to call, Peltola, Dan S. Sullivan, and Daniel J. Sullivan vote totals
  • Juneau Empire, November 4 — candidates separated by 626 votes, thousands of absentee and early votes uncounted, Justice Department subpoenas on Election Day
  • The Alaska Story, November 4 — ranked-choice tabulation scheduled November 18, 50 percent first-choice threshold, ranked-choice repeal trailing
